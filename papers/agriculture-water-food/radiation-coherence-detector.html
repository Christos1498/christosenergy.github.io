Radiation Safety

Any experimental work with radioactive sources, however low-activity, must comply with applicable NRC regulations and institutional radiation safety protocols. Nothing in this paper suggests that any standard radiation safety practice, shielding, distance, dosimetry, or licensing, can be reduced or replaced.

Abstract

The Radiation Coherence Detector (RCD) is a proposed measurement instrument for directly testing whether a radiation field's statistical detection pattern, not its underlying nuclear emission, becomes more organized in the presence of phi-ratio crystal geometry. The RCD combines established, real radiation detection technology with a proposed statistical analysis engine computing four metrics: Compton-to-primary ratio, temporal correlation, directional anisotropy, and spectral entropy.

This is a measurement protocol, not a claim that radiation is transmuted, reduced, or destroyed. The source material is unusually explicit and careful about this distinction, stating directly that nuclear emission energies are fixed by nuclear structure and cannot be altered by surrounding geometry. This page preserves that framing throughout. None of this instrument has been built or tested.

I. What This Paper Does Not Claim

The source material states its own boundaries with unusual clarity, and this page preserves that list directly: this paper does not claim that radiation is transmuted, destroyed, or eliminated; that a companion proposed intervention device works as specified; that the underlying theoretical framework is proven at the radiation scale; that nuclear emission energies change in the presence of crystal geometry; or that any specific experimental result is guaranteed. The paper claims only that the proposed instrument can measure four statistical properties of a radiation field, and that a properly designed three-condition experiment can determine whether phi-ratio crystal geometry produces a statistically significant change in those properties. Either a positive or a null result is presented as scientifically valuable.

II. The Real Physics This Builds On

The instrument's foundation is entirely real, established radiation physics: HPGe and scintillation detectors, high-speed digitizers, and gamma spectroscopy are standard, well-documented measurement technology (Knoll, 2010; Tsoulfanidis & Landsberger, 2015; Gilmore, 2008), and gamma emission energies are correctly stated as fixed by nuclear structure, not alterable by surrounding crystal geometry. The proposal that geometry and boundary conditions can influence wave behavior is real in other established physical systems, optical cavities, waveguides, and photonic crystals (Pozar, 2011; Joannopoulos et al., 2008; Haroche & Raimond, 2006, whose cavity quantum electrodynamics work is genuine, foundational physics). Whether an analogous effect exists for ionizing radiation's statistical detection pattern, as opposed to these established optical and microwave systems, is precisely the open, untested question this instrument is designed to answer, not something the cited physics itself establishes for this specific case.

III. Instrument Architecture

The proposed instrument combines a primary gamma or X-ray detector, a high-speed digitizer, a directional detector array, radiation shielding, and a phi-ratio crystal array as the test condition. The complete detector model specifications, shielding thickness, digitizer configuration, and crystal array geometry are not disclosed in this public version. The general categories of equipment involved (HPGe or scintillation detectors, standard digitizers, standard radiation shielding materials) are all real, commercially available, and regulated equipment categories.

IV. The Experiment

The proposed experiment runs three seven-day conditions, source only, source with a non-phi-ratio crystal array (a geometry control), and source with a phi-ratio crystal array (the test condition), for 21 days total. The source specification calls for a calibrated, sealed, low-activity gamma emitter in the microcurie range, the same low-activity, legally available category of source used in university physics teaching labs (Cs-137, Co-60, or Am-241), with the paper's own text explicitly requiring that "all source handling must comply with applicable NRC regulations and institutional radiation safety protocols." This page preserves that requirement as mandatory, not optional.

V. Falsifiable Predictions

The paper proposes five falsifiable predictions (R1 through R5) comparing the four measured statistics across the three experimental conditions, each with a proposed statistical significance threshold. The complete prediction thresholds and decision matrix are not disclosed in this public version, consistent with the standing policy on unvalidated performance claims applied throughout this library; the source material's own framing, that a null result would require revision of the underlying theoretical framework and is treated as scientifically valuable in its own right, is presented here as the paper's genuine intent, not a formality.
