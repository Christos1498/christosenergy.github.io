Part I. The Missing Variable — Why Metaphysics Could Not Become Physics

1.1 The Measurement Problem

Physics is built on measurable variables: mass, charge, spin, energy, momentum, spacetime coordinates. Metaphysics has always pointed at real phenomena — phenomena that billions of people have experienced directly, that appear across every culture in human history without exception. But it has done so without measurable variables. That is the entirety of the problem.

The statement 'raise your vibration' is not meaningless. It is pointing at something real. The problem is that 'vibration' as used colloquially is not a defined physical quantity. There is no instrument that measures it, no equation that governs it, no prediction that tests it. When physics encounters an unmeasured phenomenon, it does not ignore it — it searches for the variable. The history of science is the history of finding new variables: electric charge, quantum spin, entropy, spacetime curvature. Each new variable unlocked a domain that was previously invisible. The variable that unlocks metaphysics is coherence.

1.2 What Coherence Is — Formally

Coherence is not a new concept in physics. Roy Glauber won the 2005 Nobel Prize in Physics for formalizing optical coherence theory. The concept is rigorous, well-defined, and measurable. What is new in this framework is the extension of coherence from its narrow optical definition to its role as the universal organizing variable of reality.

Formally, coherence C(x,τ) is defined as a scalar measure of phase correlation across a system:

C(x) = ∫ W(x,x′) ⟨ei(φ(x) − φ(x′))⟩ dx′

Where W(x,x′) is a weighting kernel and the expectation value represents a statistical average over phase differences. High C indicates strong phase alignment — the system's components oscillate in coordinated relationship. Low C indicates random phase — incoherence, disorder, fragmentation.
