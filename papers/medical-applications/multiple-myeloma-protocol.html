Abstract

Multiple myeloma is a plasma cell malignancy of the bone marrow that remains incurable by current standards despite dramatic advances in treatment. The introduction of immunomodulatory drugs, proteasome inhibitors, anti-CD38 antibodies, and CAR-T cell therapy has transformed myeloma from an inevitably fatal disease into a chronic, manageable condition — with median survival now exceeding 10–12 years in favorable-risk patients.

Yet the fundamental challenge remains: the disease is controlled but not eliminated. Dormant myeloma stem cells persist in bone marrow even during complete remission. This paper presents the Christos™ adjunct protocol for myeloma patients in remission or on maintenance therapy, addressing the four gaps conventional therapy leaves open: dormant myeloma stem cell persistence, immune depletion from chronic therapy, treatment neurotoxicity, and the metabolic environment that permits myeloma cell survival.

Part I: Understanding Multiple Myeloma

Biology, the myeloma stem cell problem, MRD monitoring, and current treatment landscape

The Biology of Multiple Myeloma [EP]

Multiple myeloma is a malignancy of plasma cells — the terminally differentiated B lymphocytes whose normal function is to produce antibodies. Myeloma cells damage the body through crowding out normal bone marrow cells (causing anemia and immune deficiency), activating osteoclasts while suppressing osteoblasts (causing lytic bone lesions), producing M protein that damages kidneys, and secreting cytokines that suppress normal immune surveillance.

The Myeloma Stem Cell Problem [EP]

The fundamental obstacle to cure is myeloma-initiating cells (MICs) — a small population of drug-resistant clonogenic cells that persist in the bone marrow even during deep remission. MICs can enter a quiescent state invisible to therapies targeting rapidly dividing cells. The bone marrow niche provides survival signals to myeloma cells through VLA-4/VCAM-1 adhesion (cell adhesion-mediated drug resistance) and IL-6 signaling.

Remission means the fire is contained. MRD negativity means the fire is out — at least below detection threshold. But the kindling remains. The coherence framework's most specific contribution is targeting the kindling: the dormant myeloma stem cells and the niche that shelters them. [HY]

Part II: The Christos™ Coherence Framework for Multiple Myeloma

Addressing the four critical gaps — specifically designed for patients in remission on maintenance therapy

The Four Critical Gaps [EP/HY]

Gap 1
Dormant Myeloma Stem Cell Reservoir

No current therapy reliably eliminates MICs. Extended fasting (72-hr quarterly) is the most evidence-supported method for eliminating quiescent cancer stem cells and regenerating the immune system (Cheng et al., Cell Stem Cell 2014) [EP]. Frequency sweep and curcumin are proposed to address MIC niche adhesion [HY/EP].

Gap 2
Immune System Depletion from Chronic Therapy

Years of chemotherapy, corticosteroids, and proteasome inhibitors deplete T-cell, NK cell, and normal plasma cell populations. The immune support protocol rebuilds immune coherence during the maintenance phase [EP].

Gap 3
Treatment-Induced Peripheral Neuropathy

Bortezomib causes peripheral neuropathy in 30–40% of patients. Alpha-lipoic acid, acetyl-L-carnitine, methyl B12, and local frequency therapy address neuropathy support [EP].

Gap 4
Metabolic Environment Permissive to Myeloma Survival

No targeted metabolic intervention exists in standard myeloma protocols. Myeloma cells thrive in high-glucose, insulin-driven environments. Ketogenic diet + fasting + berberine create metabolic conditions unfavorable to myeloma survival [EP].

From Steady State to Victory

"Steady state" — an engineer understands this immediately. A system where inputs and outputs have balanced; nothing accelerating in the wrong direction. For a myeloma patient who has been through induction, a stem cell transplant, nerve damage, and a year of being sidelined — steady state is not nothing. Steady state is survival. Steady state is grace.

But the engineer also knows: steady state is not equilibrium. It is a dynamic balance. The disease is still there — in static, beneath the detection threshold, waiting. The immune system is depleted. The bone marrow is fighting on multiple fronts.

Steady state is not the finish line. It is the starting point for the next phase. Forward means deeper remission. Forward means a rehabilitated immune system. Forward means MRD negativity that holds. The road runs through the 72-hour fast, the curcumin, the sauna, and the MyeloPatch. All of it together. One protocol. One direction. Forward.
