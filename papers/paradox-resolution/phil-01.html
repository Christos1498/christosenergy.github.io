Abstract

The other minds problem: we cannot know with logical certainty that any other being has conscious experience. I have direct access to my own consciousness; I infer yours from your behavior and biological similarity. But inference is not knowledge. The philosophical zombie is behaviorally identical to a conscious being but has no inner experience — how would I know? This paper dissolves the problem by challenging the foundational assumption that generates it: the privacy thesis — the assumption that consciousness is essentially private, accessible only to the subject. In the CTF, consciousness is not private. It is a field phenomenon. Each conscious being is a localized modulation of the awareness field. Fields couple through coherence. When two conscious beings interact with inter-field coherence G_inter above the detection threshold C_detect, they do not infer each other's consciousness — they directly detect it through field resonance. Empathy is not inference — it is coherence detection.

1. The Problem and Its Hidden Assumption

Descartes established the philosophical architecture: the mind is private, inner, accessible only to the subject. The body is public, accessible to all. I can observe your body — behavior, language, face — but not your mind. I infer it from analogy to my own. But probability is not knowledge. A philosophical zombie could produce all the same behavioral output with no experience at all.

The CTF challenges the founding assumption directly: the privacy thesis is not a logical necessity. It is an empirical hypothesis — and a contestable one. If consciousness is a field phenomenon rather than a substance enclosed within individual skulls, fields are not private. Fields couple. The question becomes not "can I know other minds?" but "at what coherence level does direct detection become possible?"

2. Consciousness as Field — The Coherence Detection Threshold

Define inter-subject coherence:

G_inter(A,B) = |⟨ϕ_A, ϕ_B⟩| / (‖ϕ_A‖·‖ϕ_B‖)

When G_inter exceeds C_detect, the other's conscious presence is directly perceived — not inferred. When G_inter is below C_detect, only indirect inference is available. The other minds problem has practical force only when G_inter < C_detect.

3. When the Problem Dissolves

Mystical traditions across cultures consistently report that the sense of separation between self and other dissolves in deep meditative states, peak experiences, and certain psychedelic states. This is not confusion — it is the phenomenological signature of G_inter >> C_detect. When coherence coupling is very high, the other's presence is not inferred or even perceived — it is simply present, inseparable from one's own field.

The philosopher who insists that other minds are always inferred is generalizing from the low-coherence encounter (strangers passing on a street) to all encounter. The other minds problem is real for zombies and strangers. It dissolves in love.

4. Autism as Atypical Coherence Coupling

Autism spectrum conditions involve systematic differences in social perception — in CTF terms, atypical G_inter: differences in the coherence coupling mechanism that normally enables direct detection rather than inference. The autistic person is operating in a regime where G_inter remains below C_detect in contexts where it typically exceeds it for neurotypical individuals. This reframing suggests that synchrony-based therapies (rhythm entrainment, music therapy) should improve social perception more than interventions targeting cognitive inference.

5. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Empathic accuracy correlates with G_inter not with cognitive measures: physiological synchrony should predict emotional identification accuracy better than perspective-taking ability measures.
  • Coherence training improves other-minds detection: meditation and HRV biofeedback should improve performance on tasks requiring accurate perception of others' mental states through elevated G_inter.
  • G_inter elevation produces phenomenological reports of direct presence, distinguishable from normal empathic inference.
  • Autism interventions succeed in proportion to synchrony achieved, not time spent in cognitive social skills training.

6. Conclusion

The other minds problem dissolves when the privacy thesis is challenged. Consciousness is not private — it is a field. Fields couple through coherence. When G_inter exceeds C_detect, other minds are directly detected, not inferred. The philosophical problem is a low-coherence problem extrapolated to all encounter. In high-coherence encounter, the other's consciousness is as present as one's own. There is no spooky inference. There is field resonance.

G_inter > C_detect   ⟹   Direct perception of other consciousness (not inference)
