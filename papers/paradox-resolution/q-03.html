Abstract

Schrödinger's cat thought experiment was designed as a reductio ad absurdum: if quantum superposition applies to all systems, then a cat coupled to a radioactive decay event must be simultaneously dead and alive before observation. This is absurd. The question is why it is absurd — what feature of physics prevents macroscopic superpositions from persisting. This paper proposes a resolution within the Christos Theoretical Framework. The cat is not in a superposition because it cannot be — its coherence is astronomically below the threshold for quantum superposition. Coherence scales as C(N) = exp(−N/N₀), where N is the number of particles and N₀ is a characteristic scale determined by the system-environment coupling. For a cat with N ~ 10²⁵, C_cat ~ exp(−10²¹) ≈ 0. The coherence is so small that quantum superposition becomes operationally meaningless at any timescale relevant to biology or measurement. The cat is either dead or alive — not because observation collapses the wavefunction, but because macroscopic systems cannot maintain the coherence required for superposition.

2. The Coherence Threshold

2.1 Coherence Scales Exponentially with System Size

In the CTF, the coherence of a system — its capacity to maintain quantum superpositions — scales with the number of degrees of freedom coupled to the environment:

C(N) = exp(−N / N₀)

where N is the number of particles and N₀ is the characteristic coherence scale. For quantum-isolated systems (cold atoms in ultra-high vacuum): N₀ ~ 10⁶–10⁹. For warm biological systems: N₀ ~ 10²–10⁴. For a cat: N ~ 10²⁵ particles, N₀ ~ 10⁴ (body temperature, warm biological environment):

C_cat ~ exp(−10²⁵ / 10⁴) = exp(−10²¹) ≈ 0

The coherence is not approximately zero — it is zero to any finite precision. No physical measurement could distinguish C_cat from exactly zero.

2.2 The Coherence Threshold C_crit

Define C_crit ≈ 10⁻⁶ as the coherence level below which quantum superpositions are operationally unobservable — decoherence destroys them faster than any measurement could detect them. Systems with C > C_crit can exhibit quantum interference; systems with C < C_crit are effectively classical.

3. The Measurement Problem Resolved

The measurement problem asks: why do we observe definite outcomes rather than superpositions? The coherence threshold answer: we observe definite outcomes because the measuring apparatus has C < C_crit. The cat, the Geiger counter, the experimenter — all macroscopic, all with C < C_crit. None of them can maintain a superposition. The measurement apparatus is not classical by convention — it is classical by physics. The coherence threshold is the quantum-classical boundary. This is not Copenhagen (collapse by observation). It is not many-worlds (both outcomes occur). It is ordinary quantum mechanics plus the recognition that macroscopic systems cannot coherently superpose because their environmental decoherence rate exceeds any quantum evolution rate by many orders of magnitude.

4. The Quantum-Classical Boundary Is a Coherence Transition

The quantum-classical boundary is not a fixed size, mass, or temperature — it is a coherence threshold. The CTF prediction: the maximum size of a system showing quantum interference scales with the isolation quality:

N_max ~ N₀ · ln(1/C_crit) ~ N₀ · 14

For current matter-wave interference experiments (N₀ ~ 10⁴ from molecular beam isolation), N_max ~ 10⁵ atoms. This matches the experimental frontier: the largest molecule showing quantum interference in 2019 was a ~2,000-atom molecule. Scaling to 10⁵ atoms requires substantially better isolation — a prediction for next-generation experiments.

5. Macroscopic Quantum States Without Superposition

Superconductors, superfluids, and BECs maintain quantum coherence at macroscopic scales. These systems are exceptions that prove the rule: they maintain coherence because their N₀ is very large — quantum protection through energy gaps (superconductors, superfluids) or phase-locking (BECs) dramatically suppresses decoherence. The characteristic decoherence scale N₀ for a superconductor is set by the energy gap, not by the number of atoms.

6. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Maximum molecule for quantum interference: The coherence threshold predicts interference will become unobservable for molecules above N ~ N₀ · ln(1/C_crit) ≈ 10⁵ atoms under current isolation conditions.
  • Decoherence rate scales exponentially with N: The decoherence rate Γ(N) = Γ₀ · exp(N/N₀) should be measurable across a range of molecule sizes — testable by measuring coherence time as a function of molecular mass in controlled vacuum conditions.
  • No macroscopic superpositions: No experiment will ever observe a cat-scale superposition. This is a falsifiable null prediction: if cat-scale superpositions are observed, the coherence threshold model is falsified.
  • Coherence threshold is environment-dependent: The same molecule may show quantum behavior in ultra-high vacuum and classical behavior in air. N₀ should correlate with environmental coupling — measurable by systematically varying vacuum pressure or temperature in matter-wave experiments.

7. The Cat Is Safe From Superposition

The cat is not in a superposition of dead and alive. The quantum mechanics is not wrong — it is applied at the wrong scale. The radioactive atom is in a superposition. The Geiger counter, at N ~ 10²° atoms, is not. The amplification step converts a quantum superposition into a classical probability — not because consciousness collapses the wavefunction but because the amplification chain passes through systems whose coherence is zero long before it reaches the cat. Schrödinger's paradox dissolves at the coherence threshold.

C_cat ≈ exp(−10²¹) ≈ 0   ⟹   Cat is classical   ⟹   No superposition possible
