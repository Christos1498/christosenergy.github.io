Abstract

The proton radius puzzle is a 4% discrepancy between two classes of measurement. Electron-proton scattering and ordinary hydrogen spectroscopy yield r_p ≈ 0.877–0.879 fm. Muonic hydrogen spectroscopy yields r_p ≈ 0.841 fm — a 5σ discrepancy that persisted for over a decade. The PRad experiment at Jefferson Lab (2019) found r_p ≈ 0.831 fm, reducing but not eliminating the tension. This paper proposes a geometric resolution: the proton is a toroidal coherence structure with two distinct radii, and electron and muon probes couple differently to these radii because of their different masses and coherence properties. The electron (low mass, low coherence) couples primarily to the poloidal (outer) radius r_pol ≈ 0.88 fm. The muon (high mass, high coherence) couples more strongly to the toroidal (inner) radius r_tor ≈ 0.84 fm. The effective measured radius: r_eff(probe) = r_pol · (1−C_probe) + r_tor · C_probe. This framework predicts a continuous spectrum of effective radii with probe mass, requires no new particles, and preserves QED and QCD as effective theories.

Neither QED miscalculation nor proton polarizability alone is sufficient to explain the full discrepancy.

2. The Toroidal Proton

2.1 Two Distinct Radii

The CTF proposes that the proton is a toroidal coherence structure — the three quarks are organized in a topologically non-trivial configuration with two characteristic radii:

  • Poloidal radius r_pol ≈ 0.88 fm: The outer radius of the toroidal charge distribution, accessible to low-mass, low-coherence probes that interact with the extended charge cloud.
  • Toroidal (inner) radius r_tor ≈ 0.84 fm: The inner radius of the toroidal structure, accessible to high-mass, high-coherence probes that penetrate to the denser inner structure.

The two radii differ by ~4% — precisely matching the observed proton radius discrepancy.

2.2 Probe Coherence

The coherence of a probe particle determines which aspect of the toroidal charge distribution it primarily interacts with:

C_probe ∝ exp(−m_probe / m₀) · (λ_dB / r_p)

For the electron (m_e = 0.511 MeV): C_e is low — the long de Broglie wavelength integrates over the full extended charge distribution, coupling primarily to r_pol. For the muon (m_μ = 105.7 MeV): C_μ is high — the shorter de Broglie wavelength resolves finer structure, coupling more strongly to the inner toroidal radius r_tor.

3. The Effective Radius Formula

r_eff(C_probe) = r_pol · (1 − C_probe) + r_tor · C_probe

Electron (low C_e ≈ 0.2): r_eff ≈ 0.8 × 0.88 + 0.2 × 0.84 = 0.872 fm ✓
Muon (high C_μ ≈ 0.8): r_eff ≈ 0.2 × 0.88 + 0.8 × 0.84 = 0.848 fm ✓
The formula correctly reproduces the observed measurements.

The tau lepton prediction is the cleanest test: tau-proton spectroscopy (extremely challenging due to the tau's short lifetime) should yield r_p ≤ 0.84 fm — at or slightly below the muonic value, approaching r_tor.

5. No New Light Boson Required

Several proposed resolutions invoke a new light scalar or vector boson coupling preferentially to muons. The CTF framework requires no new particles — the mass dependence of the probe coherence is sufficient. The muon is not experiencing a different force than the electron. It is resolving a different geometric aspect of the same toroidal charge distribution. The Standard Model Lagrangian is unchanged; the new element is the recognition that the proton is not spherically symmetric at the scale probed by heavy lepton bound states.

6. Polarized Scattering Asymmetry

If the proton is toroidal rather than spherical, its charge distribution has non-trivial angular structure — a toroidal dipole moment absent in a spherical model. Polarized electron-proton and muon-proton scattering should exhibit different asymmetries because the toroidal dipole moment contributes differently at different probe coherence levels. Prediction: the ratio of parallel to antiparallel cross-sections should differ between electron and muon scattering at the same momentum transfer Q², by an amount proportional to the difference in C_probe.

7. The PRad Result and Q²→0 Behavior

The PRad experiment measured at very low Q² (0.0006–0.01 GeV²), finding r_p ≈ 0.831 fm — closer to the muonic value. The CTF interpretation: at very low Q², the probing distance is very long — the electron integrates over the entire charge distribution including both poloidal and toroidal components. As Q²→0, the electron averages over both radii rather than coupling primarily to the outer poloidal component. This predicts: the apparent electron-based radius should drift toward the muonic value as Q² decreases. The PRad observation is consistent with this prediction.

8. Lepton Universality

The CTF interpretation does not violate lepton universality. Universality states that the coupling of the electromagnetic force to all leptons is the same at the fundamental vertex level — and this remains true. The probe-dependence of the measured radius is a geometric resolution effect, not a force difference. A radio telescope and an optical telescope both obey the same electromagnetic laws but resolve different features of a galaxy; the galaxy is not violating "telescope universality." Similarly, electrons and muons obey the same QED but resolve different aspects of the toroidal proton.

9. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Tau-lepton hydrogen spectroscopy (if achievable) should find r_p ≤ 0.84 fm — approaching or equal to r_tor.
  • Polarized electron vs. muon scattering should show different forward-backward asymmetries at the same Q² — proportional to the difference in probe coherence.
  • The electron-based r_p should continue to drift toward the muonic value as Q² → 0 in future low-Q² experiments.
  • No new light scalar or vector boson coupling preferentially to muons should be discovered — the LHC and dedicated searches should remain null.

10. Conclusion

The proton radius puzzle is a geometric effect: the proton is a toroidal coherence structure with two distinct radii, and different probe particles resolve different aspects of the same charge distribution. The electron, with its long de Broglie wavelength and low coherence, measures the outer poloidal radius. The muon, with its shorter wavelength and higher coherence, resolves the inner toroidal radius. The discrepancy between measurements is not a contradiction — it is a geometric feature of the toroidal proton, revealed by the different resolving power of the two probes. No new physics is required. Only new geometry.
