Phase-Space Redistribution, Environmental Filtering, and Hidden Primordial Lithium

Abstract

The cosmological lithium problem is one of the oldest persistent discrepancies in modern cosmology. Standard Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN), calibrated to CMB-measured baryon densities, predicts primordial Li-7 abundances approximately 2.5–3 times greater than observed in metal-poor halo stars — the Spite Plateau. This paper proposes a coherence-based interpretation: a fraction of primordial lithium may occupy dynamically inaccessible or observationally filtered regions of early-universe phase space. The CTF identifies phase-space filtering as a Saturnalia return mechanism: lithium that entered the low-coherence return channel of the early baryon distribution is not observable through stellar spectroscopy. The framework predicts environmental variation in primordial lithium retention, correlation with local structure history, enhanced scatter at extremely low metallicities, and systematic differences between stellar, interstellar, and high-redshift absorption measurements.

Standard BBN predicts (Li/H)BBN ≈ (5.0–6.0) × 10⁻¹°. The Spite Plateau observations yield (Li/H)obs ≈ (1.6–1.7) × 10⁻¹° — a robust, repeatedly confirmed factor of ~3 deficit.

2. Phase-Space Filtering in the Early Universe

2.1 The Return Channel Interpretation

The CTF framework treats the post-BBN baryon distribution as a toroidal circulation: the Christos current carries baryons into the high-density regions (proto-galaxies, metal-poor halo stars) where we observe them; the Saturnalia return current carries baryons into the low-coherence, low-density environments (voids, diffuse filaments, phase-space regions inaccessible to stellar formation or subsequent observation). Lithium, as the lightest metal and a trace species, is particularly sensitive to phase-space redistribution during early structure formation.

2.2 Environmental Coherence and Lithium Retention

Define the lithium retention fraction as a function of local coherence:

f_Li(C_local) = exp(−α_Li / C_local)

In high-coherence regions (deep gravitational potential wells): f_Li → 1 (full retention). In low-coherence regions (diffuse, void-adjacent environments): f_Li < 1 (partial loss to return channel). The metal-poor halo stars formed in environments of intermediate coherence. Their observed lithium abundance is f_Li × (primordial abundance), consistent with the factor ~3 deficit if f_Li ≈ 0.3–0.4 for typical halo formation environments.

3. Predictions

3.1 Environmental Variation

The strongest prediction: lithium abundance among metal-poor stars should show environmental dependence beyond what stellar depletion models predict. Stars formed in higher-coherence environments should retain higher primordial lithium. The Spite Plateau should not be truly flat — it should show systematic variation with environment proxy variables.

3.2 Scatter at Extremely Low Metallicity

At [Fe/H] < −3.5, environmental filtering would produce greater variation in primordial lithium retention. The Spite Plateau should show increasing scatter below [Fe/H] ≈ −3.0, as individual formation histories become more important relative to the ensemble average. This is an unambiguous prediction distinct from stellar depletion models, which predict smaller scatter at lower metallicity.

3.3 High-Redshift Gas Reservoirs

Lithium in high-redshift intergalactic medium (z > 2) absorption systems should show the unfiltered primordial abundance — closer to the BBN prediction — because gas in diffuse IGM environments was not subject to the same phase-space transport as proto-stellar environments. JWST and ground-based high-resolution spectrographs targeting lithium absorption at high redshift should find Li/H closer to the BBN prediction than the Spite Plateau.

3.4 Globular Cluster Variation

Globular clusters of similar metallicity but different formation environments (bulge vs. halo) should show different lithium retention fractions. Bulge clusters formed in higher-coherence environments and should retain more primordial lithium. This prediction is testable with high-resolution spectroscopy of lithium in globular cluster red giant branch stars.

4. Connection to the Missing Baryons Problem

The lithium problem connects directly to the broader missing baryons problem (PR-066). Both involve baryons that BBN predicts to exist but are not directly observable in standard surveys. The CTF framework treats both as return-channel phenomena: matter that has entered the low-coherence return current of the baryon distribution and is not accessible through the standard high-coherence observational channels.

5. Falsifiable Predictions

  • The Spite Plateau should show measurable environmental variation correlated with proximity to large-scale structure features — predicted amplitude: ~0.1–0.2 dex variation across the halo population beyond measurement error.
  • Stars at [Fe/H] < −3.5 should show increasing Li scatter relative to [Fe/H] > −2.5 — a direct test distinguishing the environmental filtering model from stellar depletion models.
  • High-redshift Li absorption systems in quasar spectra should show Li/H closer to the BBN prediction (5 × 10⁻¹°) — testable with ELT, GMT, TMT high-resolution spectrographs.
  • Globular clusters in bulge vs. halo environments matched in metallicity should show systematically different Li abundances.

6. Limitations and Caveats

  • The lithium partition coefficient α_Li and the form of f_Li(C_local) are not derived from first principles — they require a detailed model of lithium transport during early structure formation.
  • Standard stellar depletion models may be sufficient to explain the deficit if systematic errors in opacity, mixing, or diffusion have been underestimated.
  • The high-redshift lithium prediction is technically challenging: Li absorption at cosmological distances is weak and difficult to observe.

7. Conclusion

The cosmological lithium problem may reflect not the failure of BBN but the phase-space filtering of primordial lithium during early structure formation. The metal-poor halo stars that define the Spite Plateau formed in environments of intermediate coherence — not the highest-density proto-galactic concentrations, where lithium would be fully retained, nor the diffuse voids, where stars do not form. A fraction of primordial lithium entered the return channel of the baryon distribution before stellar incorporation. The Spite Plateau is a filtered sample of the primordial inventory, not the complete record. Standard BBN may be correct. The stars may be telling the truth. The lithium that is missing may simply be in the channel we have not yet looked in.
