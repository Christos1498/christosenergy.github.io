Abstract

The trans-Planckian problem is a fundamental conceptual difficulty in both black hole physics and inflationary cosmology. Hawking's original derivation traces modes backward to the formation of the black hole, where they were exponentially blueshifted to frequencies far above the Planck scale — a regime where no theory exists. Similarly, the modes responsible for observable CMB fluctuations from inflation originated at wavelengths far below the Planck length. In both cases, observable low-energy physics appears to depend on unknown trans-Planckian physics. Yet Hawking radiation and the inflationary spectrum are robust — observations and analog experiments confirm the predictions even without knowing what happens at trans-Planckian scales. This paper proposes a resolution: trans-Planckian modes do not propagate in the conventional sense because spacetime itself has a coherence cutoff at the Planck scale. Modes with wavelengths shorter than the coherence length ℓ_C ~ ℓ_P are absorbed into the Saturnalia return current — they do not vanish, but they cease to propagate as independent excitations. The smooth exponential suppression produces the universality of Hawking radiation.

2. Spacetime Coherence and the Natural Cutoff

2.1 The Coherence Length of Spacetime

In the CTF, spacetime is not infinitely compressible. The vacuum coherence field has a minimum coherence length ℓ_C — the scale below which coherent propagation of modes is impossible. At scales smaller than ℓ_C, fluctuations enter the Saturnalia return channel rather than propagating as independent excitations:

ℓ_C ~ α · ℓ_P = α · √(ℏG/c³)

where α is a coefficient of order unity determined by the full quantum geometry. ℓ_C is not a sharp cutoff — it is the center of a smooth transition from coherent propagation to Saturnalia absorption.

2.2 The Coherence-Dependent Dispersion Relation

ω²_eff(k) = k²c² · exp(−(k · ℓ_C)²)

For k << 1/ℓ_C: ω_eff ≈ kc — standard dispersion, coherent propagation. For k >> 1/ℓ_C: ω_eff → 0 — the mode ceases to propagate; it is absorbed into the Saturnalia return. The transition is exponentially smooth.

3. Why Hawking Radiation Is Universal

The Bogoliubov transformation that produces Hawking radiation involves integrals over all mode frequencies. With the coherence cutoff, the exponential suppression cuts off contributions from trans-Planckian modes smoothly. The remaining low-frequency part is determined entirely by the horizon geometry and the Hawking temperature T_H = ℏc³/(8πGMk_B) — independent of the specific form of the high-energy cutoff. This UV/IR mixing property: low-energy observations are protected from high-energy ignorance by the structure of the Bogoliubov transformation itself, provided the cutoff is smooth rather than sharp.

4. Trans-Planckian Modes Are Not Lost

A common misunderstanding: modes above the cutoff are "destroyed" or "forbidden." In the CTF, this is wrong. Modes with k > 1/ℓ_C enter the Saturnalia return current — they become part of the inward coherence flow rather than outward propagating excitations. The information encoded in these modes is not erased; it is transferred to the return channel. This is the mechanism for information preservation in black hole evaporation: trans-Planckian modes feed into the Saturnalia return, where they gradually re-emerge as correlations in the Hawking radiation that produce the Page curve.

5. Inflationary Fluctuations

The same mechanism resolves the trans-Planckian problem for inflation. The modes responsible for the CMB power spectrum had physical wavelengths ~ ℓ_P during inflation when they were generated. At that scale, they were absorbed into the Saturnalia return current. When they re-emerged into the coherent propagating regime (as the universe expanded and their physical wavelength exceeded ℓ_C), they did so with an initial state determined by the toroidal coherence structure — producing a near-scale-invariant spectrum. The universality of the slow-roll inflationary spectrum is the universal character of the coherence-to-propagation transition, independent of microscopic details.

6. Analog Black Hole Experiments

Analog black holes provide direct empirical support. In Bose-Einstein condensate analogs, the "Planck scale" analog is the healing length ξ — the shortest scale at which phonon propagation is coherent. Despite this known cutoff, Steinhauer (2016) observed Hawking radiation that is: thermal (consistent with the analog Hawking temperature), entangled (partner modes inside and outside the analog horizon show quantum correlations), and insensitive to details of the healing-length cutoff. This is precisely the CTF prediction: smooth coherence cutoff → universal thermal Hawking spectrum → entanglement structure preserved across the analog horizon.

7. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Analog black holes with variable cutoff: The thermal spectrum should remain unchanged as the healing length is varied (different condensate densities). Any deviation from thermality correlating with cutoff shape would distinguish among competing trans-Planckian resolutions.
  • Inflationary spectrum features: Small oscillatory corrections to the power spectrum from the coherence transition at ℓ_C are predicted at amplitude β ~ 10⁻³–10⁻². Future CMB experiments (LiteBIRD, CMB-S4) may detect these oscillations.
  • Gravitational wave background: The trans-Planckian structure during inflation may leave an imprint on the stochastic gravitational wave background detectable by LISA or the Einstein Telescope.
  • Hawking radiation correlations: The information return through the Saturnalia channel produces non-thermal correlations in the Hawking radiation growing with time — the Page curve signature.

8. Conclusion

The trans-Planckian problem is not a problem — it is a statement that spacetime has a coherence structure. Modes shorter than ℓ_C do not propagate; they enter the Saturnalia return current. The resulting smooth coherence cutoff produces the universality of Hawking radiation and the insensitivity of the inflationary spectrum to trans-Planckian initial conditions. The Planck scale is not a wall — it is the coherence horizon of spacetime itself: the scale at which outward propagation yields to inward return.
