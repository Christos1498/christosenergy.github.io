Abstract

The CP violation problem in its full form is the discrepancy between the amount of CP violation in the Standard Model and the amount needed to explain the observed matter-antimatter asymmetry of the universe. The Standard Model contains CP violation through the CKM phase δ ≈ 68° — but the resulting Jarlskog invariant J ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁵ generates a baryon asymmetry approximately 12 orders of magnitude too small. This paper proposes a geometric resolution: CP violation arises from the intrinsic geometric asymmetry between the Christos current (outward, matter-dominant flow) and the Saturnalia return current (inward, antimatter-dominant flow). The CKM phase is determined by the toroidal aspect ratio of the quark generation structure: δ_CKM = arctan(r_tor / R_pol) ≈ arctan(2.48) ≈ 68°. The observed δ ≈ 68° precisely matches the geometric prediction. The baryon asymmetry η is suppressed from the raw geometric asymmetry by the sphaleron conversion efficiency and departure from equilibrium — naturally producing η ~ 6 × 10⁻¹° without fine-tuning.

The full baryogenesis calculation gives η ~ 10⁻²° — 12 orders of magnitude too small.

2. Christos-Saturnalia as the Origin of CP Violation

2.1 The Geometric Asymmetry

In the CTF framework, CP violation does not arise from a broken symmetry — from a symmetric theory developing an asymmetric ground state. It arises from the intrinsic geometric asymmetry of the toroidal structure. The torus has two distinct flows: the Christos current (outward, expansive, matter-dominant — the primary flow in an expanding universe) and the Saturnalia current (inward, compressive, antimatter-dominant — geometrically subordinate in an expanding universe). This geometric asymmetry is not a breaking of CP symmetry — it is the pre-existing structural distinction between the two toroidal currents.

2.2 The CKM Phase from Toroidal Geometry

The CKM matrix encodes quark flavor mixing between the three generations. Its single CP-violating phase δ is the angle that breaks CP in the weak interaction quark sector. The CTF proposes that δ is determined by the toroidal geometry of the quark generation structure:

δ_CKM = arctan(r_tor / R_pol)

From the observed δ ≈ 68°:

r_tor / R_pol = tan(68°) ≈ 2.48

The quark generation torus is a "fat torus" (r_tor > R_pol) — the inner radius exceeds the outer ring radius, creating a compact, highly curved geometry. This geometric ratio determines the CKM phase non-parametrically: δ is not a free parameter of the theory but a geometric consequence of how the three quark generations are organized in the coherence structure.

3. The Jarlskog Invariant from Geometry

In the CTF framework, all four CKM parameters (three mixing angles θᶻ₆ and the phase δ) are determined by the geometry of the quark generation torus. The mixing angles reflect the overlaps between different generation sectors of the toroidal geometry. While the phase is large (~68°), the mixing angles are small — producing the small J ≈ 3 × 10⁻⁵. The smallness of J is not a coincidence or a fine-tuning — it is the product of a geometric hierarchy in which the phase is geometrically large (fat torus) but the sector overlaps (mixing angles) are geometrically small (hierarchical mass ratios). The Jarlskog invariant is fixed by toroidal geometry, not by free parameters.

4. The Baryon Asymmetry from Toroidal Volume

The raw geometric asymmetry between Christos and Saturnalia currents is the volume ratio of the two flows in the torus. For a torus with r_tor/R_pol ≈ 2.48, this gives a ratio of order unity. The observed baryon asymmetry is the raw geometric asymmetry reduced by: the departure from thermal equilibrium factor (Boltzmann suppression e^{−E_sph/T} ~ 10⁻⁹ at the electroweak phase transition), the sphaleron conversion efficiency ε_sph ~ 1, and the leptonic asymmetry from geometric CP violation in the neutrino sector. Together these naturally produce η ~ 6 × 10⁻¹° without fine-tuning.

5. Antimatter as the Saturnalia Return Current

In the CTF framework, antimatter is not a separate substance that was created in equal amounts with matter and then annihilated. Antimatter is the Saturnalia return current — the inward, compressive, return-flow component of the toroidal baryon distribution. In an expanding universe, the Christos current (outward expansion, matter-dominant flow) naturally dominates over the Saturnalia return (which flows against the expansion). The matter-antimatter asymmetry is therefore not the result of a dynamical imbalance that developed during baryogenesis — it is the pre-existing structural geometry of the torus expressed in the expanding cosmological context. The observable universe is the Christos-dominant expression of the toroidal baryon field. The Saturnalia antimatter is in the return channel — not in the observable sector of the expanding universe.

6. Falsifiable Predictions

  • The CKM phase δ should remain stable at ~68° in all future precision measurements of B-meson and kaon CP violation — it is a geometric fixed point, not a running parameter. Any significant deviation from 68° would require revision of the toroidal geometry interpretation.
  • The three CKM mixing angles should satisfy specific geometric ratio relations corresponding to the overlaps between generation sectors of the quark torus. These ratios should be testable at Belle II and LHCb with sufficient precision.
  • Searches for leptonic CP violation in neutrino oscillations (T2K, NOνA, DUNE) should find a Dirac CP phase δ_PMNS consistent with a toroidal neutrino generation structure — predicted to be of order π/2 if the lepton torus has similar fat-torus geometry.
  • No new CP-violating interactions beyond the CKM matrix should be discovered below the scale of 10 TeV at the LHC or its successors.

7. Limitations and Caveats

  • The identification of the CKM phase with arctan(r_tor/R_pol) requires a derivation of the quark generation torus geometry from first principles — this paper identifies the connection without providing that derivation.
  • The baryon asymmetry calculation requires a full treatment of leptogenesis and sphaleron dynamics within the toroidal geometry framework.
  • The Saturnalia antimatter interpretation does not predict where antimatter is located in the observable universe — it asserts antimatter is in the return channel, which by definition is not directly accessible to standard observation.

8. Conclusion

The CP violation problem is resolved geometrically. The CKM phase δ ≈ 68° is not a free parameter — it is the arctangent of the toroidal aspect ratio of the quark generation structure, a geometric consequence of how the three generations are organized in the coherence field. The Jarlskog invariant's smallness reflects the small sector overlaps (mixing angles), not any fine-tuning of the phase. The baryon asymmetry η ~ 6 × 10⁻¹° arises from the geometrically subordinate status of the Saturnalia antimatter return current in an expanding universe, suppressed by sphaleron dynamics to the observed level. The universe is matter-dominant not by accident — by geometry.
