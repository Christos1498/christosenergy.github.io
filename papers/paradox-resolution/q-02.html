Abstract

The Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen paradox and Bell's theorem together establish one of the most profound facts in physics: quantum mechanics is nonlocal in the sense that entangled particles exhibit correlations that no local hidden variable theory can explain. Experiments by Aspect, Zeilinger, Hensen, and others have closed all major loopholes, confirming Bell inequality violations at extraordinary precision. The question is not whether they exist — it is what they mean. This paper proposes a resolution within the Christos Theoretical Framework. The EPR correlations are not nonlocal influences — they are phase co-location: entangled particles share a common phase configuration in toroidal coherence space. In 3D space, they are separated. In coherence space, they are co-located at Δϕ = 0. The apparent "action at a distance" is a coordinate artifact of projecting a coherence-space co-location onto a 3D spatial description.

2. Phase Co-Location in Coherence Space

2.1 Spatial Separation as Phase Difference

In the CTF, spatial separation between objects is not fundamental — it is the macroscopic expression of phase difference in the underlying coherence field. Two objects at different locations have different phases in the coherence field. For unentangled particles, Δϕ is large and ΔS is the ordinary spatial distance. For maximally entangled particles in the singlet state, Δϕ = 0 in coherence space — they are phase co-located despite being spatially separated.

2.2 What Phase Co-Location Means

Phase co-location does not mean the particles are physically at the same 3D location. It means they are at the same location in coherence space. The 3D spatial positions are emergent from the coherence geometry, and entangled particles are co-located in the more fundamental coherence-space description while being separated in the emergent 3D description. The correlation between measurement outcomes is not transmitted when the measurements are made — it is the pre-existing fact of their coherence-space co-location.

2.3 Why No Signaling

No faster-than-light signaling is possible because measurement on particle A does not change particle B's coherence-space location — both particles are already co-located. The measurement on A reveals the pre-existing phase configuration. Classical communication is required to verify the correlation because neither observer knows the shared phase configuration without comparing results — but the correlation itself requires no signaling.

3. Bell Inequality Violations as Coherence-Space Geometry

The standard Bell inequality assumes that measurement outcomes at A and B are determined by local variables in 3D space. In coherence space, both measurements access the same shared phase configuration — not a local variable at each location but a global coherence-space property. The coherence-space correlation function:

E(a,b) = cos(θ_ab) · C_entanglement

For perfect entanglement (C_entanglement = 1), E(a,b) = cos(θ_ab), recovering the quantum prediction that violates the Bell bound. The violation is the geometric consequence of accessing a shared phase configuration rather than independent local variables.

4. Decoherence and the Emergence of Locality

As entangled particles interact with their environments, the shared phase configuration is disturbed. Environmental coupling creates phase differences: Δϕ grows from 0 toward large values as decoherence proceeds. The effective spatial separation in coherence space increases. When Δϕ is large, the particles are effectively independent in coherence space — local correlations emerge. Classical locality is the limit of complete decoherence. This explains why macroscopic objects appear local: their continuous environmental interaction destroys coherence-space co-location and produces the 3D spatial independence we call locality.

6. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Bell violation magnitude correlates with entanglement coherence: As decoherence is introduced via controlled environmental coupling, the Bell violation S should decrease as C_entanglement decreases. S = 2√2 · C_entanglement. Testable in any quantum optics or ion trap experiment with controlled noise.
  • Entanglement decay rate correlates with environmental coupling strength: The rate at which Δϕ grows (decoherence rate) should match the bath spectral density, testable in cavity QED and NV center experiments.
  • Multipartite phase co-location: For N-particle GHZ states, the violation of N-particle Bell inequalities should scale with coherence as S_N = 2^{(N−1)/2} · C^N.
  • Phase co-location gives smooth partial violations: The full spectrum of partial violations should follow S = 2√2 · C_entanglement with no threshold — a smooth function rather than a sharp transition.

7. Conclusion

The EPR paradox is resolved by recognizing that spatial separation is not the fundamental level of description. In coherence space, entangled particles are co-located at Δϕ = 0. The Bell correlations do not require signaling — they are the pre-existing fact of a shared phase configuration. The violations of Bell inequalities are not mysterious — they are the geometric signature of accessing a global coherence-space property rather than independent local variables. Einstein was right that something was missing from quantum mechanics — but it was not hidden variables. It was coherence space. There is no spooky action at a distance. There is only phase identity, misread as spatial separation.

Entanglement: Δϕ_AB = 0  ⇔  ΔS_coherence = 0  ⇔  Phase co-location
