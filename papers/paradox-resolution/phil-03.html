The Sorites Paradox

Coherence Thresholds, Phase Transitions, and the Physics of Vagueness

Abstract

The sorites paradox (the paradox of the heap): one grain of sand is not a heap; adding one grain to a non-heap never produces a heap; therefore no collection of grains is ever a heap. Yet heaps exist. The conclusion contradicts obvious experience. The problem generalizes to every vague predicate — bald, tall, old, red — which together constitute a major portion of natural language semantics. Standard responses (epistemicism, supervaluationism, degrees of truth) each handle parts of the phenomenon at significant theoretical cost. This paper proposes a resolution requiring no revision to classical logic: vague predicates correspond to coherence thresholds — the points at which a system crosses from one phase to another. "Heap" names an emergent phase in which a sand collection has achieved sufficient organizational coherence (characteristic angle of repose, stable surface geometry, collective avalanche behavior) to be a unified structured entity rather than merely an aggregate. Premise 2 is false: adding one grain sometimes does cross the coherence threshold and produce a heap.

1. The Paradox and Its Teeth

The sorites paradox has three features that make it genuinely difficult: (1) both premises seem true — one grain is clearly not a heap, and clearly one grain never makes the critical difference; (2) the argument form is valid — if both premises are true, the conclusion follows by mathematical induction; (3) the conclusion is clearly false — heaps exist and we reliably identify them.

The problem generalizes to every gradable predicate: "tall," "bald," "rich," "old," "alive." These are not peripheral vocabulary — they constitute a major portion of natural language semantics. A solution to the sorites is a solution to the nature of vagueness itself.

2. Heaps Are Phases, Not Aggregates

The key move: "heap" does not describe an arbitrary collection above a grain count threshold. It describes a specific physical phase — the phase in which a granular material has achieved organized collective behavior.

PropertyMere aggregateHeap phase
Angle of reposeNot defined — grains lie flatCharacteristic angle (varies by material)
Surface stabilityUnstable — no coherent surfaceStable — coherent surface geometry
Collective dynamicsNone — grains independentCollective avalanche behavior
Field coherenceLow — grains not coupledHigh — collective organization established

The sorites premise "adding one grain never makes a heap" is false in exactly the same way that "adding one water molecule never produces wetness" is false. At the critical density, one additional molecule does trigger the phase transition.

3. The Coherence Threshold Resolution

Define C_heap as the coherence threshold for the heap phase. The sorites argument fails because Premise 2 is false: adding one grain at the critical configuration DOES cross C_heap.

The reason Premise 2 seems compelling: in most of the range (well below or well above the threshold), adding one grain changes nothing perceptible. The phase transition is local — it occurs at a specific configuration. For 99.9% of grain counts, adding one grain does nothing dramatic. This makes the premise feel universally true when it is only almost universally true — false specifically at the threshold.

Vague predicateCoherence threshold
"Heap"C_heap (granular organizational coherence)
"Bald"C_hair (hair coverage sufficient for coherent field)
"Alive"C_life (metabolic coherence threshold)
"Conscious"C_cons (neural coherence threshold — B-03)

4. Context Dependence as Threshold Shift

"Heap" is context-dependent: a heap of rice and a heap of boulders involve very different grain counts. Context shifts the threshold. What counts as "sufficient organizational coherence for heap" depends on the material (its angle of repose, grain size, density). What counts as "sufficient height advantage" depends on the population being compared to. Context sets the threshold; the threshold determines which phase the system is in.

5. Falsifiable Predictions

  • The heap/non-heap transition is sharp in coherence space, fuzzy in grain-count space: under controlled conditions, transition should show a sharp threshold in angle-of-repose measurements but a fuzzy threshold in grain count.
  • Sorites-susceptible predicates correlate with physical phase transitions: predicates producing the strongest Premise 2 intuition should be precisely those whose referents involve real phase transitions with identifiable transition regions.
  • Context-shifting changes threshold coherence, not threshold logic: height judgments should remain internally consistent within a context even as they shift across contexts.

6. Conclusion

The sorites paradox is resolved by recognizing that vague predicates name coherence thresholds, not arbitrary grain counts. Premise 2 fails at the threshold. Vagueness is not a logical deficiency — it is the linguistic signature of phase transition proximity: the accurate description of a system in the transition region between two phases.

Sorites dissolves: Premise 2 fails at C_heap. Vagueness = transition region proximity.

Resolution Framework — The Five Moves

Move AppliedApplication to This Paradox
Move 2: Coherence ThresholdC_heap is the threshold that separates the aggregate phase from the heap phase. The sorites argument attempts to deny any threshold by claiming Premise 2 is universally true. It is almost universally true — false only at the threshold. Identifying the threshold makes the falsity precise and physically grounded.
Move 3: Correct ScaleThe sorites argument operates at the scale of individual grains. The heap phase exists at the scale of collective organization. At the grain scale, Premise 2 is almost always true. At the collective-organization scale, it is false at the threshold.
