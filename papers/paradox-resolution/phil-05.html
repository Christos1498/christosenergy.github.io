Abstract

Hume's is/ought problem (1739): no description of how things are entails how things ought to be. From purely factual premises, no evaluative conclusion validly follows. This gap has been foundational for meta-ethics. The CTF proposes a resolution that does not violate Hume's logical insight but shows that the gap exists only within a stripped-down metaphysics that excludes the coherence structure of the universe. In the CTF, the universe has not only material properties but also coherence properties — the organizational structure of its field. Coherence has a gradient: ∇C points in the direction of increasing coherence. This gradient is a fact about the universe — an "is" — but a special kind: a directional fact about which configurations sustain themselves and which decay. The "ought" is derived not from a neutral "is" but from a directed "is": the direction of coherence gradient is the direction of sustainable flourishing.

1. Hume's Gap — Why It Is Real

Hume's observation is logically impeccable. No set of purely descriptive premises logically entails any evaluative conclusion. The gap cannot be closed by collecting more facts. No amount of factual detail, stated in purely descriptive terms, logically entails any evaluative conclusion. This is why Hume's insight survived two centuries of naturalistic attempts to close it. The logical gap is real.

2. What Hume's Metaphysics Excluded

Hume recognized only: (a) mathematical/logical properties (relations of ideas) and (b) empirical properties (matters of fact). Value properties do not fit either category — hence the gap.

The CTF expands the metaphysical inventory with a third category: directional field properties — properties that are empirical (measurable) and contingent but inherently directional rather than neutral. Coherence is the paradigm case. The coherence gradient ∇C is an empirical property of the universe — it points in a specific direction in configuration space — and it is not a neutral fact. It is a directed fact: configurations with higher C sustain themselves; configurations with lower C decay.

4. Ethical Dilemmas as Multi-Scale Coherence Conflicts

Ethical dilemmas arise when coherence gradients at different scales point in different directions. The CTF does not pretend these conflicts dissolve easily. It provides a framework: identify the relevant scales, identify the coherence gradients at each scale, and seek the action that maximizes coherence across the highest relevant scale while minimizing coherence loss at lower scales. The action most coherent at the longest timescale and broadest scope is typically the more ethical action, even if it incurs short-term or local coherence costs.

5. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Cross-cultural ethical convergence tracks coherence-increasing actions: actions judged ethical across the largest number of independent moral traditions should be precisely those that are coherence-increasing across the widest range of relevant scales simultaneously.
  • Coherence measurement predicts moral intuition: when experimental participants are given moral judgment scenarios, their judgments should correlate with independently assessed coherence impacts of the described actions across cultures.
  • Ethical disagreement concentrates at multi-scale conflicts: the most intractable moral disagreements should be precisely those where coherence gradients conflict across scales — predicting that these disagreements are real (not merely cultural) and that resolution requires identifying the appropriate higher-order attractor.

6. Conclusion

Hume's guillotine cuts only in a universe of neutral facts. In a universe with a coherence field — whose organizational structure has a direction, a gradient, a distinction between configurations that sustain themselves and configurations that decay — the gap narrows to something crossable. The "ought" is derived from the direction the universe's field organization points — and that direction, measured at every scale, is toward greater coherence, greater sustainability, greater organizational capacity for further flourishing. Ethics is not opposed to physics. Ethics is the human practice of navigating the coherence gradient wisely.
