Abstract

Abstract

The naturalness problem asks why the parameters of the Standard Model are hierarchically small relative to their natural values. The Higgs mass is 10^{17} times smaller than the Planck scale. The strong CP phase is below 10^{-10}. The electron Yukawa coupling is 3×10^{-6}. Standard solutions — supersymmetry, technicolor, axions — remain experimentally unconfirmed. This paper proposes a geometric interpretation within the Christos Theoretical Framework: naturalness is not a problem but a consequence of toroidal compactification geometry. The hierarchy between the electroweak scale and the Planck scale is encoded in the toroidal aspect ratio ε = r_tor/R_pol, and all small Standard Model parameters are powers of this ratio. This is an interpretive framework, not a completed derivation — the aspect ratio ε is currently fitted to observations rather than derived from first principles, and a derivation from the toroidal vacuum geometry remains the outstanding theoretical task. The framework nevertheless generates falsifiable predictions: no SUSY partners at LHC energies, exact strong CP phase zero, and Yukawa coupling ratios following powers of ε.

1. The Naturalness Problem

Standard Model parameters violate the naturalness criterion — dimensionless parameters not protected by symmetry should be of order unity. Several observed parameters deviate from this by many orders of magnitude:

ParameterObserved
m_H / M_Pl (Higgs-Planck ratio)~10^{-17}
θ_QCD (strong CP phase)< 10^{-10}
y_e (electron Yukawa)~3×10^{-6}
Λ_CC / M_Pl^4 (cosmological constant)~10^{-120}

2. The Toroidal Aspect Ratio Interpretation

In the CTF, small dimensionless parameters are interpreted as powers of the toroidal aspect ratio:

ε = r_tor / R_pol

The Higgs mass sets the scale: m_H/M_Pl ~ ε gives ε ~ 10^{-17}. With this value, the electron Yukawa y_e ~ ε² ~ 4×10^{-34} (observed: ~3×10^{-6} — requires additional generation structure factors not yet computed). The QCD scale Λ_QCD/M_Pl ~ ε^{1/2} ~ 4×10^{-9} (observed: ~10^{-9} — consistent). The strong CP phase is exactly zero by toroidal vacuum quantization (companion paper PR-075).

The cosmological constant problem is reduced but not resolved: the CTF suppression ε^4 ~ 10^{-68} falls 52 orders of magnitude short of the observed 10^{-120}. This gap is acknowledged openly.

3. Status of the Framework

The aspect ratio ε is currently a phenomenological parameter — it is chosen to reproduce the Higgs-Planck hierarchy, then checked against other parameters. It is not yet derived from a more fundamental calculation of the toroidal vacuum geometry. This is the central theoretical limitation of the framework in its current form.

The electron and muon mass predictions require additional factors beyond simple powers of ε — the generation structure of toroidal wavefunction overlaps needs explicit calculation. The pattern is approximately right at the order-of-magnitude level but not precisely derived.

The cosmological constant remains the most severe naturalness problem. No known extension of the CTF resolves it. This is stated explicitly rather than minimized.

4. What the Framework Does Establish

Despite the limitations above, the geometric interpretation makes three testable claims that are independent of the specific value of ε:

  • No SUSY partners at LHC energies: If the hierarchy is geometric rather than symmetry-based, supersymmetric partners are not required for stability. The HL-LHC should continue to find no superpartners. This is a genuine prediction, not merely consistency with null results.
  • Strong CP phase exactly zero: The toroidal vacuum quantization forces θ_QCD = 0 exactly (PR-075). Next-generation neutron EDM experiments (n2EDM, TUCAN) should find no signal above the geometric correction level ~10^{-27} e·cm.
  • Yukawa hierarchy follows a pattern: The three-generation Yukawa hierarchy should follow a Froggatt-Nielsen-like pattern with ε as the small parameter. Precision Yukawa coupling measurements at future lepton colliders provide a test once the generation-structure factors are computed.

    • 5. Limitations and Outstanding Tasks

  • ε is fitted, not derived. A first-principles derivation from the toroidal vacuum geometry — computing r_tor and R_pol from the quantum vacuum energy and topological constraints — is the primary outstanding theoretical task.
  • The electron and muon Yukawa predictions require explicit calculation of toroidal wavefunction overlaps for each generation. The order-of-magnitude pattern is encouraging; exact predictions require the full calculation.
  • The cosmological constant problem is reduced from 120 to 52 orders of magnitude by ε^4 — but 52 orders remains an enormous unexplained gap. The framework does not solve this problem.
  • Predicted KK excitations at ~246 GeV with very small couplings have not been observed. This constrains the coupling strength but does not falsify the framework if couplings are sufficiently suppressed.

    • 6. Conclusion

    The naturalness problem is reinterpreted as a geometric consequence of toroidal compactification. The Higgs-Planck hierarchy encodes the toroidal aspect ratio. The strong CP phase is forced to zero by topological quantization. The Yukawa hierarchy follows an approximately correct pattern. These are genuine insights even given the acknowledged limitation that ε is currently a phenomenological parameter rather than a derived one. The framework is a research program, not a completed theory. The outstanding task — deriving ε from first principles — is clearly defined and tractable.

    ε = r_tor/R_pol [currently fitted; derivation is the outstanding theoretical task]
