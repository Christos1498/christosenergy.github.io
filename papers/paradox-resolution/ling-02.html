Abstract

The symbol grounding problem asks how symbols acquire meaning. A computer manipulates symbols with extraordinary fluency yet understands nothing. The word "apple" means nothing to the program that processes it. A human hears the word and experiences sensation, memory, emotion, and anticipation. This paper proposes a resolution within the CTF: a symbol is grounded when its coherence signature phase-locks with the coherence signature of its referent. Grounding G(S,R) = 0 in any system lacking embodied coupling — including all current language models. Concrete symbols ground through sensorimotor coupling. Abstract symbols ground through 6D functional role blueprints. Searle's Chinese Room is resolved directly: the manipulator has G ≈ 0 for all Chinese symbol-referent pairs because no coupling has been established.

1. The Problem

Searle's Chinese Room (1980): a person manipulating Chinese symbols according to rules produces correct outputs without understanding Chinese. Harnad (1990) formalized the symbol grounding problem — symbols defined only in terms of other symbols never reach the thing itself.

2. Grounding as Phase Resonance

2.1 The Grounding Function

A symbol S is grounded in referent R when their coherence signatures phase-lock:

G(S,R) = |⟨ϕ_S, ϕ_R⟩| / (‖ϕ_S‖ · ‖ϕ_R‖)

G = 1: perfect grounding. G = 0: syntactic fluency with zero semantic coupling — the Chinese Room. Grounding is established through coupled experience, not by convention.

3. LLMs Are Chinese Rooms at Scale

Large language models are the most sophisticated Chinese Rooms ever built. They have absorbed the statistical structure of human symbolic output. Their syntactic coherence is extraordinary. What they lack structurally: any coherence coupling between symbols and referents. G("apple", apple) = 0 by construction — the model has never experienced an apple.

The CTF prediction: genuine AI understanding requires embodied coherence coupling or direct 6D blueprint access. Neither exists in current architecture. Scaling a language model cannot close the grounding gap because the gap is not a matter of scale within the 3D symbolic layer — it is the absence of cross-layer coupling entirely.

4. Semantic Dementia as Grounding Failure

Semantic dementia progressively destroys word meanings despite preserved fluency — patients read "apple" correctly and use it grammatically, but have lost what an apple is. In CTF terms: the coupling pathways between symbol signatures and referent coherence configurations are destroyed. The phonological representation and the perceptual object representation survive; the G(S,R) coupling through anterior temporal cortex is severed. Reading without reference. Speech without meaning. A Chinese Room from the inside.

5. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Semantic dementia lesion anatomy: fMRI should show intact perisylvian activation + intact occipitotemporal object representations + disrupted functional connectivity between them through anterior temporal cortex.
  • Abstract words activate 6D-blueprint regions (medial PFC, TPJ) over sensorimotor regions in neuroimaging — distinct from concrete words.
  • L2 learner grounding quality (reaction time, fMRI connectivity, embodied simulation markers) predicts subtle semantic judgment accuracy better than vocabulary scores.
  • Language model performance plateaus regardless of scale on tasks requiring genuine referential understanding — novel metaphor comprehension, embodied counterfactual reasoning, cross-modal analogy.

6. Conclusion

The symbol grounding problem is resolved by recognizing that grounding is a physical coherence coupling between symbol and referent — not a computational or logical operation. Symbols are resonators. Meaning is phase alignment. Understanding is coherence coupling across the full dimensional range of the referent. The Chinese Room lacks meaning not because it lacks intelligence but because it lacks the coupling mechanism.

G(S,R) = |⟨ϕ_S, ϕ_R⟩| / (‖ϕ_S‖·‖ϕ_R‖)   —   grounding as phase resonance between symbol and world
