Abstract

The angular momentum problem is a persistent discrepancy between cosmological simulations and observations of disk galaxies. Simulations systematically underpredict the specific angular momentum j = J/M of disk galaxies by factors of 1.25–2.0 across the observed j−M relation. This paper proposes a geometric resolution: standard gravity simulation codes model gravity as a purely radial force, omitting the tangential component g_θ that drives spiral circulation in the CTF framework. This spiral gravity torque acts continuously during galaxy formation: τ_spiral = ∫(r × g_θ) dV, where g_θ = −(GM/r²) sin α, and α is the gravitational pitch angle. When spiral gravity is included, galaxies naturally acquire the specific angular momentum observed. The framework predicts that angular momentum scales with spiral arm pitch angle, connecting galaxy morphology directly to angular momentum acquisition.

2. The Tangential Component of Gravity

2.1 Spiral Gravity

Within the CTF framework, gravity in the presence of spiral mass distributions is not purely radial. The gravitational field of a logarithmic spiral distribution has both radial and tangential components:

g(r,θ) = −(GM/r²)(cos α r̂ + sin α θ̂)

where α is the gravitational pitch angle. For α = 0: standard Newtonian radial gravity. For α > 0: a tangential Christos current appears, continuously accelerating the gas and stars in the direction of orbital motion. Standard N-body and SPH codes solve Poisson's equation for radial gravity; the spiral torque term is not captured unless the mass distribution explicitly develops spiral arms in the simulation.

2.2 The Spiral Torque

The torque exerted on a mass element dm by the tangential gravity component:

dτ = r × g_θ dm = −(GM/r) sin α dm

Integrating over the forming disk:

τ_spiral = −∫ (GM(r)/r) sin α(r) ρ(r) dV

This torque acts throughout galaxy formation. For a galaxy forming over t_form ~ 5 Gyr with mean sin α ~ 0.3, the spiral torque contribution to j is comparable to or exceeds the tidal torque contribution from large-scale structure — which is the sole angular momentum source in standard models.

4. The j∘−M∘ Slope

The CTF predicts:

j∘ ∝ M∘^{2/3} · ⟨sin α⟩(M∘)

If more massive galaxies have smaller pitch angles (tighter spirals, more evolved), the product M∘^{2/3} · ⟨sin α⟩ may deviate from pure M∘^{2/3} — predicting a slight bending of the j∘−M∘ relation at the high-mass end consistent with the observed transition from spirals to lenticulars.

5. High-Redshift Prediction

At high redshift (z > 1), galaxies are smaller, more irregular, with less-developed spiral structure. The mean pitch angle α(z) decreases with increasing z as spirals are less well-developed. Therefore the angular momentum deficit should be larger at high redshift than locally. JWST NIRSpec kinematic surveys at z ~ 1–2 combined with morphological pitch angle measurements can test whether the angular momentum deficit scales with observed pitch angle across cosmic time.

6. The j_b/j_DM Ratio

Observed disk galaxies have baryonic specific angular momentum j_b ~ 2–3× higher than dark matter halos. Standard simulations produce j_b/j_DM ≈ 1 without extreme feedback tuning. The CTF predicts a naturally higher j_b/j_DM because spiral gravity acts most strongly on the baryonic disk component, which responds dissipatively and develops the spiral structure generating the torque. Dark matter, being collisionless, responds less efficiently to the tangential torque. Baryons are spun up preferentially; dark matter retains its tidal-torque-derived j. The ratio j_b/j_DM ~ 2–3 emerges naturally from the differential coupling of baryons and dark matter to the spiral torque.

7. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Pitch angle–angular momentum correlation: j∘ should correlate with sin α at fixed M∘ across a statistically large sample of nearby spiral galaxies. Testable with SDSS morphological catalogs + kinematic surveys.
  • High-z angular momentum deficit larger than local: at z ~ 1, the simulation-observation j∘ ratio should be further from 1 than at z = 0, because spiral structures are less developed and the spiral torque contribution is smaller. Testable with KMOS, VLT/MUSE, JWST NIRSpec kinematics.
  • Sc galaxies should show the largest improvement when spiral torque is added to hydrodynamic simulations — the predicted improvement scales with sin α ≈ sin(30°) ≈ 0.5.
  • The j_b/j_DM ratio should be larger for galaxies with more open spiral arms (larger α) — testable with combined kinematic and morphological surveys.

8. Conclusion

The angular momentum problem is resolved by recognizing that gravity in a spiral mass distribution is not purely radial. The tangential component of gravity in a spiral potential acts as a continuous Christos current, spinning up the disk throughout its formation history. Standard simulations omit this torque because they do not explicitly resolve spiral arm formation during the early disk assembly phase. When spiral gravity is included, galaxies naturally acquire the specific angular momentum observed without requiring exotic feedback tuning. Galaxy morphology and angular momentum are geometrically linked — the pitch angle is the angular momentum gauge of spiral structure.
