Abstract

General anesthesia is 170 years old and its mechanism remains incompletely understood. Structurally diverse drugs — inhaled gases, propofol, ketamine, barbiturates, xenon — produce the same endpoint: reversible unconsciousness with reliable recovery. They act on different molecular targets (GABA-A, NMDA, potassium channels) yet converge on the same phenomenology. This convergence demands a common pathway. This paper proposes that the common pathway is coherence suppression within the Christos Theoretical Framework. Consciousness requires sustained Christfield activity above a coherence threshold: X > X_cons, where X = δC/δΨ is the Christfield gradient. Anesthetics reduce X below X_cons by disrupting phase synchronization across neural assemblies — reducing the brain's global coherence C_brain. Different anesthetics produce different coherence suppression profiles but share the common endpoint of C_brain < C_cons. The framework predicts that coherence monitoring is the correct measure of anesthetic depth; the coherence threshold C_cons is individual-specific; recovery is governed by coherence restoration speed; and disorders of consciousness are chronic sub-threshold coherence states potentially reversible by coherence-restoring interventions.

2. Consciousness as Christfield Above Threshold

In the CTF, consciousness requires the Christfield gradient X = δC/δΨ to exceed a threshold X_cons. The brain coherence:

C_brain = (1/N(N−1)) ∑_{i≠j} |PLV_ij|

where PLV_ij is the phase-locking value between EEG channels i and j, measures the global phase synchronization across neural assemblies. Anesthesia produces a measurable decline in C_brain below C_cons. Loss of consciousness occurs not at a specific drug plasma concentration but at a specific coherence threshold — which is why anesthetic requirements vary so dramatically between individuals.

3. Anesthetic Depth as Coherence Depth

Current anesthetic depth monitors (BIS, PSI) measure EEG power spectra. The CTF predicts that coherence-based monitoring is more fundamental:

Depth(t) = C_cons / C_brain(t)

When C_brain(t) >> C_cons: inadequate anesthesia (depth < 1). When C_brain(t) = C_cons: threshold (depth = 1). When C_brain(t) << C_cons: deep anesthesia (depth > 1). Coherence-based depth should better predict intraoperative awareness (the most feared anesthetic complication), emergence delirium, and optimal drug dosing for specific individuals.

4. Recovery as Coherence Restoration

Propofol plasma half-life is 3–8 minutes for short cases. Yet patients often emerge within seconds of drug discontinuation. EEG coherence recovers faster than drug elimination kinetics predict from pharmacokinetics alone. The CTF explanation: once the drug concentration falls below the molecular threshold, the brain's intrinsic coherence mechanisms (neural oscillators, gap junctions, bioelectric coupling) rapidly restore C_brain above C_cons. Recovery is not drug elimination — it is coherence restoration. The coherence restoration speed predicts clinical emergence speed better than drug half-life.

5. Disorders of Consciousness as Chronic Sub-Threshold States

Vegetative state (VS) and minimally conscious state (MCS) patients have C_brain chronically near or below C_cons. The distinction between VS and MCS may be a coherence level distinction: VS patients have C_brain < C_cons with no fluctuations above threshold; MCS patients have C_brain fluctuating around C_cons — occasionally crossing into consciousness. This framework predicts that coherence-enhancing interventions (TMS, DBS, zolpidem) that push C_brain above C_cons, even transiently, should produce clinical responses — consistent with reported cases.

6. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Coherence threshold C_cons varies between individuals and predicts awareness risk: Pre-induction EEG coherence should predict awareness probability — testable in large prospective surgical cohort.
  • Coherence monitoring outperforms BIS: In a head-to-head comparison for preventing intraoperative awareness and guiding drug dosing, coherence monitoring should show superiority — testable in a randomized controlled trial.
  • Sub-threshold VNS restores consciousness in MCS: Vagal stimulation that enhances coherence without producing motor-level activation should improve behavioral responsiveness in MCS patients correlated with coherence increases.
  • Ketamine's dissociative anesthesia reflects regional coherence disruption: High-density EEG during ketamine anesthesia should show thalamocortical coherence suppression while corticocortical coherence remains higher.

7. Conclusion

The mystery of general anesthesia dissolves when consciousness is understood as a coherence threshold phenomenon. All anesthetics reduce C_brain below C_cons through different molecular pathways — but the common endpoint is always the same: the Christfield drops below the threshold for phenomenal experience. Recovery is coherence restoration. Anesthetic depth is coherence depth. Individual variation in anesthetic requirement is individual variation in the coherence threshold. Disorders of consciousness are chronic sub-threshold states. Anesthesia is not a chemical mystery — it is coherence management.
