Abstract

Music's emotional power is universal, cross-cultural, and incompletely explained. A sequence of organized sounds can induce joy, grief, terror, and transcendence reliably across individuals and cultures who share no common musical conditioning. This paper proposes a resolution grounded in established acoustic physics, psychoacoustics, and cardiac coherence research: music is a coherence modulation tool. Specific frequency ratios directly entrain the listener's neural and physiological coherence fields. Simple integer ratios (2:1 octave, 3:2 fifth, 4:3 fourth) produce strong phase locking across brain and body — felt as consonance and positive valence. More complex ratios introduce phase offset — felt as tension or melancholy. Rhythm entrains cardiorespiratory rhythms, with tempo-HRV coupling producing maximum cardiac coherence. The emotion is the coherence configuration the music induces in the listener's field.

Simple integer ratios produce beat frequencies harmonically related to the original frequencies — self-reinforcing acoustic structures. Complex ratios produce inharmonic beat frequencies that create conflicting neural synchronization patterns.

The major chord (4:5:6 ratio structure) produces coherence expansion across all three voices simultaneously — a Christos configuration: outward, expansive, felt as positive valence. The minor chord (10:12:15 ratio) introduces phase offset at the third — a Saturnalia inflection: inward, compressive, felt as melancholy or depth.

3. Rhythm, HRV, and Physiological Coherence

HRV coherence (the sine-wave oscillation of heart rate at respiratory frequencies) is the most accessible proxy for whole-body autonomic coherence. The HeartMath Institute has documented HRV coherence responses to music across hundreds of studies (McCraty et al. 1996, 2010).

  • Slow tempos (50–60 bpm) matching respiratory-cardiac coupling: enhance RSA, increase HRV coherence, produce parasympathetic dominance — felt as calming and opening.
  • Tempos near resting heart rate (60–80 bpm): entrain cardiac rhythm, produce moderate HRV coherence — felt as emotionally engaged.
  • Fast tempos (>100 bpm): drive locomotion and sympathetic activation — felt as energizing, activating.

4. Falsifiable Predictions

  • HRV coherence peaks during consonant passages and drops during dissonant passages — with the effect preceding conscious emotional report by at least 200 ms.
  • Heart rate entrainment to musical tempo is strongest when tempo is within 10% of the listener's resting heart rate.
  • Major-mode music produces higher inter-brain EEG coherence than minor-mode music in simultaneous dual-EEG recordings.
  • Cross-cultural consonance preference correlates with physiological HRV coherence response to consonant vs. dissonant intervals — linking the behavioral universal to the physiological mechanism.

5. Conclusion

Music and emotion are coherence modulation. Consonant intervals increase field coherence — felt as pleasure and positive valence. Dissonant intervals fragment coherence — felt as tension. Minor intervals introduce Saturnalia inward pull — felt as melancholy and depth. Rhythm entrains physiological coherence — felt as the body being moved. The profoundly musical experience is not metaphor: it is the literal experience of one's coherence field being reorganized by acoustic phase coupling.
