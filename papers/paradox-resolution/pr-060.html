Abstract

The abc conjecture, formulated by Masser and Oesterlé in 1985, is one of the most powerful and far-reaching conjectures in number theory. For coprime positive integers a + b = c, it states that for any ε > 0, there are only finitely many triples with c > rad(abc)^{1+ε}, where rad(n) is the product of distinct prime divisors of n. The conjecture implies Fermat's Last Theorem asymptotically, the Mordell conjecture, and effective Siegel bounds, among dozens of other results.

This paper proposes a geometric interpretation within the toroidal coherence framework. The abc conjecture is a coherence capacity bound on additive relations: the radical rad(abc) measures the arithmetic coherence of the triple (its information-theoretic diversity in prime factors), while c measures the output of the additive relation. When the radical is small — meaning many prime factors are repeated (high compression, low diversity) — the additive structure a + b = c cannot produce a large c relative to that compressed base. The conjecture says that arithmetic coherence has finite capacity: you cannot obtain more from an addition than the prime diversity of the inputs supports.

1. The Conjecture

For coprime positive integers a, b, c with a + b = c, define:

rad(abc) = ∏_{p | abc} p   (product of distinct prime divisors)

The abc conjecture: for every ε > 0, there exists a constant C_ε such that c ≤ C_ε · rad(abc)^{1+ε} for all such coprime triples. Equivalently, the quality q(a,b,c) = log(c)/log(rad(abc)) satisfies q ≤ 1 + ε for all but finitely many triples.

2. The Radical as Arithmetic Coherence

2.1 Radical and Coherence Deficit

The CTF framework reframes the radical as an arithmetic coherence measure. A triple (a,b,c) with small radical means that many prime factors appear with high multiplicity — arithmetic compression: the primes that divide abc are few but they appear many times. High radical means high diversity (many distinct primes); low radical means low diversity (few primes, many repetitions).

Define the arithmetic coherence of a triple:

C(a,b,c) = log(c) / log(rad(abc))

The abc conjecture states C(a,b,c) ≤ 1 + ε for all but finitely many triples. This is a coherence capacity bound: the ratio of output (log c) to diversity (log rad) cannot be unboundedly large.

2.2 Why Coherence Has Capacity

The Christos current in this framework is the additive structure — the outward expression a + b = c. The Saturnalia return is the multiplicative structure — the prime factorizations that constrain what a, b, and c can be. The abc conjecture says the additive output cannot exceed the multiplicative return capacity. When radicals are small (few distinct primes, high compression), the multiplicative return current is weak — the Saturnalia channel is narrow. A narrow channel cannot support a large Christos output.

They cannot exceed quality 1 + ε because no arithmetic configuration can compress the additive output beyond what the multiplicative diversity supports.

4. Fermat's Last Theorem as Coherence Violation

Fermat's equation aⁿ + bⁿ = cⁿ for n ≥ 3 gives quality approximately:

q(aⁿ, bⁿ, cⁿ) ≈ n / (1 + log(rad(abc))/log(c))

For large n with fixed a, b, c: rad(aⁿbⁿcⁿ) = rad(abc) (the radical does not change under taking n-th powers — only new primes matter), while cⁿ grows as cⁿ. The quality approaches n, which for large n violates the abc bound of 1 + ε. This is why abc implies FLT asymptotically: n-th powers produce extreme coherence overextension — the additive output cⁿ is so large relative to the unchanged radical that the bound q ≤ 1 + ε cannot be satisfied. The equation aⁿ + bⁿ = cⁿ for large n demands more from the arithmetic coherence field than its multiplicative structure can supply.

5. Mochizuki's IUT as Coherence Bridge

Mochizuki's Inter-universal Teichmüller Theory (IUT) constructs a framework for analyzing the tension between multiplicative and additive structure by deconstructing and reconstructing the ring structure of integers at a fundamental level. The theta-link transfers information between different copies of the multiplicative structure; the log-theta-lattice provides the organizational framework. The CTF interpretation: IUT is a formal coherence bridge between the Christos current (additive structure, the output c) and the Saturnalia return (multiplicative structure, the radical). If correct, IUT is a formalization of coherence capacity in arithmetic.

6. Connection to Szpiro's Conjecture

Szpiro's conjecture for elliptic curves E/ℚ: |Δ(E)| ≤ C_ε · N(E)^{6+ε}, where Δ is the minimal discriminant and N is the conductor. Szpiro is equivalent to the abc conjecture. The CTF interpretation: the conductor N(E) is the arithmetic coherence measure of the curve, while the discriminant Δ(E) is the output of the elliptic curve's additive structure. Szpiro says: the arithmetic complexity of an elliptic curve cannot exceed the coherence of its reduction behavior beyond the 6+ε power. Same bound, different arithmetic object.

7. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Systematic computational search for abc triples with quality q > 1.63 should continue to return null — no triple should exceed quality 2.0 even for c up to 10³⁰ or beyond. Any triple with q > 2.0 would require re-examination of the conjecture.
  • The distribution of qualities q should thin out rapidly above q = 1.5 and approach a statistical limit below 2.0 — testable through extended computational surveys.
  • New families of high-quality triples, if found, should involve only primes with high repetition in the factorizations of a, b, c — consistent with the coherence compression interpretation.
  • If Mochizuki's IUT is eventually accepted and formalized, it should be interpretable as a coherence capacity theorem: the theta-link should be identifiable as a formal statement about the additive-multiplicative coherence bound.

8. Limitations and Caveats

  • The CTF interpretation identifies the structural reason the conjecture holds but does not provide a proof within standard number theory.
  • The coherence capacity concept requires formalization: the connection between log(c)/log(rad) and a rigorously defined arithmetic coherence measure needs mathematical development.
  • The interpretation of IUT as a coherence bridge is structural; whether IUT actually proves abc remains contested in the mathematical community.

9. Conclusion

The abc conjecture is the coherence capacity bound of arithmetic: the additive relation a + b = c cannot produce an output c that exceeds what the multiplicative prime diversity of (a,b,c) can support. The radical rad(abc) is the coherence measure — the diversity of the arithmetic field available to the addition. Large c with small radical means the additive output exceeds the multiplicative return capacity: the Christos current outpacing the Saturnalia supply. The conjecture says this overextension is always finite. Fermat's Last Theorem is the extreme case: n-th powers produce radical compression while the additive output grows, making the quality diverge for large n and ruling out integer solutions.

c ≤ C_ε · rad(abc)^{1+ε}   ⇔   Additive output ≤ Multiplicative coherence capacity
