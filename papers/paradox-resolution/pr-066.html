Abstract

The missing baryons problem is one of the longest-standing discrepancies in modern cosmology. BBN and CMB measurements constrain the baryon fraction to Ω_b ≈ 0.049. Direct censuses of stars, interstellar gas, intracluster gas, and the observed warm-hot intergalactic medium (WHIM) account for only about 35–40% of this predicted baryon budget in the local universe (z < 1). This paper proposes a geometric resolution: missing baryons are phase-separated into the Saturnalia return channel of the cosmic web, where they are compressed into low-coherence filamentary and void structures undetectable by standard X-ray and UV absorption surveys. The CTF baryon partition function predicts that the visible baryon fraction decreases from ~100% at z > 2 to ~35% at z = 0 — matching observations. The strongest falsifiable prediction: at z > 2, direct observations should account for 100% of the baryon budget with no missing fraction.

2. The Toroidal Baryon Partition

2.1 Christos and Saturnalia Baryon Channels

The CTF framework partitions the total baryon budget between the Christos current (observable: stars, cold gas, hot intracluster gas, WHIM) and the Saturnalia return channel (hidden: compressed, low-coherence structures in voids and diffuse filaments). The partition depends on the large-scale coherence of the cosmic web:

f_Christos(z, C) = exp(−α_b / (1 − C(z)))

At high redshift (z > 2): the universe was more coherent, C(z) ≈ 0.85–0.99, and f_Christos → 1. At low redshift (z < 1): structure formation has reduced coherence, C(z) ≈ 0.5, and f_Christos ≈ 0.35 — matching the observed ~35% baryon accounting.

3. The WHIM as Transitional Phase

The WHIM is not the Saturnalia return channel — it is the transitional zone between channels. WHIM filaments are baryons in the process of being transferred from the observable Christos channel to the return channel, or returning from it. The WHIM is shock-heated because it sits at the interface between the two channels — the zone of energy conversion as baryons transition between coherence states. Prediction: the WHIM baryon fraction should peak at z ≈ 0.5–1 (where the transitional zone is most active) and decline at both lower and higher redshifts — testable with XRISM and Athena X-ray spectroscopy.

4. Detecting Return-Channel Baryons

4.1 Fast Radio Burst Dispersion Measures

FRBs are millisecond radio pulses from extragalactic sources. Their dispersion measure (DM) traces the integrated electron column along the line of sight. Return-channel baryons — fully ionized, low-temperature, compressed in filamentary structures — contribute free electrons to the DM even though they are not hot enough for X-ray emission. Prediction: the DM excess (observed DM minus modeled IGM+WHIM contribution) should correlate with the predicted return-channel fraction at each redshift. CHIME, ASKAP, and DSA-2000 will localize thousands of FRBs, providing statistical precision for this test.

4.2 kSZ Effect in the Cosmic Web

Return-channel baryons may be detectable through the kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) effect — the CMB temperature shift produced by bulk motion of ionized gas along the line of sight. Cross-correlating CMB maps with large-scale structure tracers should reveal kSZ signal from cosmic web filaments including the return-channel baryon component. CMB-S4 and the Simons Observatory will reach sufficient sensitivity.

4.3 Ultra-Narrow Lyman-α Absorption

Return-channel baryons in compressed low-temperature filaments would produce Lyman-α absorption with extremely narrow Doppler widths (b < 10 km/s — below the thermal broadening of T ~ 10⁴ K gas). Standard photoionized IGM clouds have b ≈ 20–40 km/s. Ultra-narrow components, if present in high-resolution quasar spectra (ESPRESSO, future ELT/HIRES), would be the spectroscopic signature of return-channel gas.

5. High-Redshift Closure

The strongest prediction: at z > 2, f_missing → 0 and all baryons should be accounted for by direct observation. JWST and ALMA can now measure stellar masses, cold gas content, and IGM opacity at z > 2 with sufficient precision to test whether the baryon budget closes. If f_missing remains ~0.65 at z > 2, the CTF partition model is falsified.

6. Falsifiable Predictions

  • FRB dispersion measures should show redshift-dependent DM excess consistent with the return-channel fraction f_missing(z). The Macquart relation should have systematic scatter correlated with line-of-sight density of cosmic web filaments.
  • At z > 2, JWST+ALMA direct censuses of stellar mass + cold gas + IGM should account for > 80% of the baryon budget predicted by BBN/CMB — the missing fraction should decrease monotonically with redshift.
  • Cross-correlation of CMB maps with cosmic web filament catalogs should reveal kSZ signal from return-channel baryons in filaments — amplitude larger than predicted by WHIM-only models.
  • Ultra-narrow Lyman-α absorbers (b < 10 km/s) should be present in high-resolution quasar spectra above standard IGM models.

7. Limitations and Caveats

  • The partition coefficient α_b and coherence evolution C(z) are phenomenological — they require a detailed baryon transport model to derive from first principles.
  • FRB DM excess detection requires accurate modeling of host galaxy DM contributions, which currently dominate the scatter in the Macquart relation.
  • The ultra-narrow absorber prediction assumes return-channel gas is cool enough to avoid thermal broadening — the temperature of return-channel gas is not well-constrained.

8. Conclusion

The missing baryons are not uniformly distributed in the WHIM — they are phase-separated into the Saturnalia return channel of the cosmic web: compressed, low-coherence filamentary and void structures undetectable by standard surveys. The CTF baryon partition predicts a monotonic decrease in the missing baryon fraction with increasing redshift, reaching near-zero at z > 2. This is one of the cleanest predictions in the framework — directly testable with JWST, ALMA, FRB surveys, and next-generation CMB experiments. The missing baryons are not gone. They are in the return current, waiting to be measured at the right frequency, in the right direction, at the right redshift.
