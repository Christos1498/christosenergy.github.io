Abstract

The strong CP problem is the puzzling observation that QCD does not violate CP symmetry despite containing a term in its Lagrangian that should produce large CP violation. The QCD Lagrangian includes the topological θ-term: ℒ_θ = θ · (g_s²/32π²) · Gᵃ_{μν} G̃ᵃ°μν, which violates CP unless θ is zero or extremely small. Experimental limits on the neutron electric dipole moment (nEDM) constrain |θ| ≤ 10⁻¹°. The mystery: why is θ so small when it is not protected by any symmetry? The most popular solution — the Peccei-Quinn mechanism with the axion — has not yielded an axion detection. This paper proposes an alternative: θ is forced to zero by the requirement that the vacuum coherence field be single-valued around the toroidal cycles of the QCD vacuum. The effective θ-parameter: θ_eff = 2π(r_tor/R_pol − n) = 0 when r_tor/R_pol is an integer n. No axion is needed. The nEDM is predicted to remain undetected from θ, with the full contribution being exactly zero and only a tiny geometric correction remaining.

2. Toroidal Quantization of the θ-Parameter

2.1 The QCD Vacuum as Toroidal Coherence Field

In the CTF, the QCD vacuum is a toroidal coherence field (as established in PR-074). The gluon field configurations are subject to toroidal boundary conditions: field configurations must be single-valued (periodic) around the toroidal and poloidal cycles of the vacuum geometry. The topological charge:

ν = (g_s²/32π²) ∫d⁴x Gᵃ_{μν}G̃ᵃ°μν ∈ ℤ

is an integer by topology (instanton number). The θ-term in the path integral contributes a phase e^{iθν}. For the vacuum to be globally consistent (single-valued around all toroidal cycles), the accumulated phase around each cycle must be a multiple of 2π.

2.2 The Quantization Condition

The effective θ-parameter in the CTF vacuum satisfies:

θ_eff = 2π(r_tor/R_pol − n),   n ∈ ℤ

The periodicity condition requires r_tor/R_pol to be an integer (or more precisely, a rational number consistent with the topological charge quantization). The simplest consistent solution: r_tor/R_pol = n (integer) → θ_eff = 0 exactly.

2.3 Why the Aspect Ratio Is Integral

The quantization r_tor/R_pol = n follows from the requirement that the QCD vacuum sustains stable topological excitations (instantons) without fractional winding. Fractional winding would produce fractional topological charge, but topological charge is quantized in integer units by the homotopy group π₃(SU(3)) = ℤ. The toroidal geometry of the QCD vacuum must therefore be commensurate — r_tor/R_pol must be an integer for the simplest stable vacuum configuration. This is not fine-tuning — it is a topological quantization condition, as robust as the quantization of electric charge.

3. The Neutron EDM Prediction

If θ_eff = 0 exactly from toroidal quantization, the θ-term contributes exactly zero to the nEDM. The remaining contributions:

  • CKM-induced EDM through three-loop quark level calculation: d_n^{CKM} ~ 10⁻³² e·cm. Far below current sensitivity.
  • Geometric correction from imperfect toroidal symmetry (finite quark mass differences, next-order corrections): d_n^{geometric} ~ 10⁻²⁷–10⁻²⁶ e·cm. Within range of next-generation experiments.

4. Why No Axion Is Needed

The Peccei-Quinn mechanism introduces a new U(1) symmetry that is spontaneously broken, producing a pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson (the axion) that relaxes θ to zero dynamically. The CTF mechanism is fundamentally different: θ does not need to be dynamically relaxed — it is fixed at zero by a topological quantization condition. The QCD vacuum is a toroidal coherence field with integer aspect ratio; this is a static geometric property, not a dynamical process. No axion field, no axion particle, no Peccei-Quinn symmetry. The strong CP problem is resolved by the same mechanism that quantizes magnetic flux in a superconductor — topology forces the coupling to an integer multiple.

6. Falsifiable Predictions

  • The nEDM should remain below the current limit (1.8 × 10⁻²⁶ e·cm) from the θ-term contribution, but should be detected at d_n ~ 10⁻²⁷–10⁻²⁶ e·cm from the geometric correction in n2EDM at PSI (targeting 10⁻²⁸ e·cm) and TUCAN at TRIUMF.
  • No axion should be detected in ADMX, CASPEr, ABRACADABRA, or other axion search experiments across the mass range 10⁻¹²–10⁻⁵ eV — the CTF predicts no axion exists.
  • The topological susceptibility of the QCD vacuum (measurable on the lattice) should be consistent with integer winding number quantization — no fractional-winding configurations should appear in the low-energy vacuum.
  • The nEDM should show no electric field dependence beyond QED radiative corrections — no θ-induced term means no field-dependent CP violation in hadronic systems.

7. Limitations and Caveats

  • The quantization condition r_tor/R_pol = n requires r_tor/R_pol to be exactly integer. If R_pol and r_tor are determined by quantum fluctuations rather than topological quantization, the condition may be only approximately satisfied — allowing |θ| to be small but nonzero.
  • The geometric correction to the nEDM (d_n ~ 10⁻²⁷–10⁻²⁶ e·cm) requires derivation from the full CTF vacuum field theory.
  • The absence of an axion remains to be confirmed by experiment — current axion searches have not yet covered the full predicted mass range.

8. Conclusion

The strong CP problem is resolved by toroidal vacuum quantization. The QCD vacuum is a toroidal coherence field with toroidal boundary conditions that force the aspect ratio r_tor/R_pol to an integer, setting θ_eff = 0 exactly as a topological requirement. No Peccei-Quinn symmetry, no axion, no new particles. The nEDM from the θ-term is exactly zero; the remaining geometric correction of d_n ~ 10⁻²⁷–10⁻²⁶ e·cm is within reach of next-generation experiments. The strong CP problem is not a problem with CP violation being too small — it is the vacuum's topology declaring that CP must be exactly conserved in the strong sector.

θ_eff = 2π(r_tor/R_pol − n) = 0   —   topology forbids CP violation in QCD
