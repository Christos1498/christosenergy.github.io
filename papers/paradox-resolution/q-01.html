Abstract

The quantum Zeno effect is experimentally confirmed: sufficiently frequent measurements of a quantum system suppress its evolution. An unstable particle observed continuously does not decay — the "watched pot never boils." The effect has been demonstrated in trapped ions, superconducting qubits, cold atoms, and cavity QED. Standard interpretations invoke the projection postulate without providing a physical mechanism for why observation halts evolution. This paper proposes a coherence-locking interpretation within the Christos Theoretical Framework. Measurement is not an abstract operation — it is a physical coherence event. When a measurement is performed, the system's phase becomes locked to the measuring apparatus. This coherence lock resets the quantum phase to a definite value, interrupting the phase decoherence that drives evolution. The Zeno-to-anti-Zeno transition at critical interval τ_c is determined by the coherence spectrum of the system-bath interaction.

1. The Effect

For a quantum system in initial state |0⟩ evolving under a Hamiltonian with transition rate Ω, the short-time survival probability is:

P_surv(τ) ≈ 1 − (Ωτ)²

After N measurements at interval τ, total time T = Nτ:

P_surv(T,τ) = [1 − (Ωτ)²]^{T/τ} → 1 as τ → 0

2. The Coherence Locking Interpretation

2.1 Measurement as Physical Coherence Event

In the CTF, a measurement is a physical interaction that establishes coherence between the system and the measuring apparatus. When the measurement is complete, the system's phase is locked to the apparatus state. This coherence lock resets the phase coherence parameter C(t) to its maximum value. Define the system coherence:

C(t) = |⟨ψ(0)|ψ(t)⟩|² = 1 − (Ωt)² + O(t⁴)

A measurement resets C to 1 — the coherence lock. Between measurements, C decays quadratically. The survival probability:

P_surv(T,τ) = [C(τ)]^{T/τ} → 1 as τ → 0

2.2 The Anti-Zeno Effect as Coherence Acceleration

When the measurement interval τ matches the correlation time of the environment τ_c, the measurement-induced coherence reset creates a state optimally coupled to the bath decay modes — accelerating evolution rather than suppressing it. The transition from Zeno (suppression) to anti-Zeno (acceleration) at τ ~ τ_c is a coherence resonance:

τ_c = 1 / ∫ J(ω) dω

where J(ω) is the bath spectral density. This is directly measurable: τ_c can be determined from the system-bath coupling spectrum independently, and the Zeno-to-anti-Zeno transition should occur precisely at this interval.

3. Zeno Subspaces as Coherence-Protected Domains

When measurement frequency >> transition rates, the accessible Hilbert space collapses to a Zeno subspace H_Z — the set of states reachable by the measurement-compatible dynamics. In CTF terms: H_Z is the high-coherence subspace of the full Hilbert space. Measurement locks the system into this high-coherence domain, preventing exploration of lower-coherence states that would constitute evolution or decay. Evolution within H_Z is allowed — this is already used in quantum error correction: measurements continuously project the system into the logical qubit subspace, suppressing all error channels that would take the system outside it.

4. Quantum Non-Demolition as Continuous Coherence Locking

Quantum non-demolition (QND) measurement preserves the measured observable while gaining information. In the coherence-locking picture, QND is the τ → 0 limit of Zeno measurement: the coherence is continuously locked to a fixed value, the system evolution within the compatible subspace continues, and no information is destroyed. This explains why QND does not violate the Zeno effect while still providing measurement information — it locks the relevant coherence while leaving the orthogonal evolution free.

5. Imperfect Measurements and Partial Coherence Locking

The standard Zeno effect assumes ideal projective measurement (perfect coherence lock). Real measurements are imperfect — detector efficiency η < 1. In the CTF, imperfect measurement produces partial coherence locking. The effective Zeno survival probability with detector efficiency η:

P_surv(T,τ,η) ≈ exp(−T · Ω² · τ · (1−η)²)

This is directly testable by varying detector efficiency in existing ion trap or superconducting qubit experiments.

6. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Imperfect measurement weakens Zeno: Survival probability should decrease as detector efficiency decreases, following the η-dependent formula above. Testable in any high-fidelity qubit platform.
  • Anti-Zeno transition at τ_c: The critical interval should match the bath correlation time determined independently from spectroscopy. Testable in qubit-bath systems with tunable bath spectral density.
  • QND coherence advantage: QND measurement should preserve the measured observable's coherence longer than equivalent projective measurements, by a factor proportional to the coupling-bandwidth product.
  • Zeno subspace protection correlates with coherence: Systems with higher baseline coherence should show stronger Zeno suppression for the same measurement interval.

7. Conclusion

The quantum Zeno paradox is reinterpreted as coherence locking: measurement is a physical coherence event that resets the system's phase to a definite value, interrupting the phase drift that constitutes evolution. The watched pot does not boil because measurement continuously locks the coherence — not because the observer's knowledge halts the physics. This interpretation produces precise, testable corrections to standard Zeno predictions for imperfect measurements and provides the physical mechanism behind Zeno subspaces and quantum non-demolition.

P_surv(T,τ) = [C(τ)]^{T/τ} → 1 as τ → 0   —   coherence locking freezes evolution
