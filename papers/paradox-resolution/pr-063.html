Abstract

The small-scale structure problem — the missing satellites problem — is a persistent tension between ΛCDM simulations and observations of low-mass galaxy populations. High-resolution N-body simulations predict substantially more low-mass dark matter subhalos around galaxies like the Milky Way than are observed as luminous dwarf satellite galaxies. This paper proposes a structured interpretive framework centered on environmental realization thresholds and selective luminous emergence within hierarchical structure formation. The core proposal: many predicted low-mass subhalos exist dynamically while failing to cross the environmental and baryonic thresholds required for observable luminous realization. The coherence framework identifies these thresholds as coherence phase transitions — the minimum organizational coherence required for gas accretion, cooling, and star formation to proceed. Subhalos below the coherence threshold for star formation are dark — not absent.

The Milky Way is predicted by ΛCDM simulations to have hundreds to thousands of dark matter subhalos with masses sufficient to host luminous dwarf galaxies. Observations identify approximately 50 confirmed satellite galaxies within 300 kpc. The discrepancy spans 1–2 orders of magnitude at the low-mass end and cannot be fully resolved by observational incompleteness alone.

2. The Coherence Threshold for Star Formation

2.1 Star Formation as a Threshold Phenomenon

The CTF framework treats star formation as a coherence threshold crossing: a subhalo must achieve sufficient organizational coherence — measured by gas cooling efficiency, gravitational potential depth, and freedom from external radiation field — to convert baryons into stars. Below the threshold C_SF, no stars form regardless of dark matter mass:

ε_SF(M, C_orbital) = ε₀ · Θ(C(M, orbit) − C_SF)

2.2 Why Small Subhalos Are Dark

The reionization epoch (z ~ 6–10) bathed the universe in ultraviolet radiation from early galaxies. Small halos with V_max < 20–25 km/s could not retain gas against this photoionizing background — their shallow potential wells allowed baryon loss. In CTF terms: reionization reduced the coherence of small subhalos below C_SF by destroying the cold gas that would have enabled star formation. These subhalos became permanently dark — not because they failed to form, but because their organizational coherence was suppressed below the luminosity threshold before stars could form.

2.3 Orbital Coherence

Beyond mass, orbital properties determine subhalo coherence. Define: C_sub = ⟨cos(Δϕ)⟩ / (1 + e), where ⟨cos(Δϕ)⟩ is the mean phase alignment with the galactic toroidal field and e is orbital eccentricity. Subhalos on high-coherence orbits (near-circular, in-plane) may cross C_SF; subhalos on low-coherence orbits (polar, eccentric) remain below C_SF despite mass. The prediction: observed luminous satellites should be preferentially on high-coherence orbits.

3. The Dark Satellite Population

The CTF framework predicts that the "missing" satellites are present as dark matter subhalos — not absent. They are accessible through:

  • Gravitational lensing: Dark subhalos at ~10⁷–10⁹ M☉ produce measurable flux anomalies in strongly lensed systems. JWST, Euclid, and Roman Space Telescope are designed to detect these.
  • Stellar stream perturbations: Dark subhalos passing through or near stellar streams (GD-1, Pal 5, Anticenter Stream) produce gaps, wiggles, and density variations. Observed stream gap frequency should match the CDM subhalo mass function.
  • Proper motion anomalies: A population of dark subhalos passing through the Milky Way disk would leave detectable signatures in local stellar proper motion statistics — measurable with Gaia extended mission data.

4. Milky Way vs. Andromeda Coherence Difference

The Milky Way occupies the edge of the Local Void — a large underdense region with lower average environmental coherence. Andromeda (M31) is embedded deeper in the Local Group's overdense region. This environmental difference predicts: the Milky Way should have fewer luminous satellites relative to dark satellites than Andromeda; Andromeda should have a higher fraction of satellites above C_SF. Comparing the luminosity functions of the two galaxies' satellite populations, corrected for distance effects, provides a direct test. PAndAS (Pan-Andromeda Archaeological Survey) has mapped Andromeda's satellite population for this comparison.

5. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Gravitational lensing surveys (Euclid, JWST) should detect dark subhalos in Milky Way-mass halo lenses at CDM-predicted abundance. Detection of a significant dark subhalo population would confirm the "dark not absent" interpretation.
  • Gaia proper motions and RVS radial velocities should show that confirmed luminous Milky Way satellites have systematically lower eccentricities and smaller inclinations than expected for a random sample of CDM subhalos.
  • Andromeda should have a higher luminosity function normalization at the faint end relative to the Milky Way, corrected for distance-dependent incompleteness.
  • Stream gap statistics should be consistent with the CDM subhalo mass function at masses 10⁶–10⁹ M☉, without requiring suppression of subhalo abundance.

6. Limitations and Caveats

  • The coherence threshold C_SF is not derived from first principles — it must be calibrated against observed satellite luminosity functions and star formation histories.
  • Multiple mechanisms (feedback, reionization, tidal stripping, warm dark matter) likely all contribute to satellite suppression; the CTF framework adds environmental coherence as an organizational framework rather than a unique mechanism.
  • The "dark not absent" prediction is falsifiable in principle but requires next-generation surveys for definitive test.

7. Conclusion

The small-scale structure problem is not a crisis for ΛCDM — it is a realization filtering problem. The dark matter subhalos predicted by ΛCDM exist. A large fraction of them are dark because they fell below the coherence threshold for star formation during reionization or because their orbital coherence was insufficient to accumulate the gas and organizational capacity needed for luminous realization. The missing satellites are not missing — they are dark, gravitationally present, and accessible through the next generation of surveys designed to detect them. The resolution is not a modification of dark matter physics. It is a recognition that luminosity is not guaranteed by mass — coherence is the gatekeeper.
