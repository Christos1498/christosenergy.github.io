Abstract

Hume's problem of induction (1739): no amount of observed instances can logically justify the expectation that the next instance will follow the same pattern. The sun has risen every day in human history — this gives no logical guarantee it will rise tomorrow. Any attempt to justify induction inductively is circular. This paper proposes that both Hume's problem and Goodman's new riddle of induction dissolve within the CTF. Induction works because coherence persists — the universe's coherence field does not vanish discontinuously, so past phase configurations reliably predict near-future phase configurations. The new riddle resolves because coherent predicates track real phase boundaries in the coherence field while grue-like predicates are coordinate artifacts with no field reality.

1. Why Hume Was Right — And What He Missed

Hume identified a genuine logical gap. No finite set of observations logically entails any universal generalization. This is not fixable by collecting more observations — the gap is logical, not empirical. No amount of confirming instances closes it.

What Hume missed: the question is not only about logical entailment. It is about physical structure. The universe is not logically required to be coherent. But it is physically coherent. And in a coherent universe, the logic of induction is not what does the epistemic work. The physics of coherence persistence does.

2. Coherence Persistence — Why the Future Resembles the Past

In the CTF, the universe maintains a coherence field whose phase organization changes continuously rather than discontinuously. In a coherence-maintaining system, the phase configuration at t+dt is highly predictable from t — because coherence persistence means the field changes slowly and continuously.

3. Where Induction Fails — Coherence Phase Transitions

The most important prediction of the coherence persistence account: induction fails at phase transitions — exactly where we observe it to fail. Near a phase transition, C(t) → 0 and the future configuration is uncorrelated with the past. The 2008 financial crisis was a phase transition in financial system coherence — every predictive model based on pre-transition data failed simultaneously. The Bronze Age collapse (~1200 BCE) was a phase transition in Mediterranean civilizational coherence.

4. Goodman's New Riddle — Coherent vs. Grue Predicates

Goodman (1955) introduced "grue": an object is grue if it is green and observed before time t, or blue and observed after time t. All observations of emeralds before t confirm both "all emeralds are green" and "all emeralds are grue" equally. Why does only the first support the next-emerald prediction?

CTF answer: "green" tracks a real phase boundary in the coherence field (the absorption spectrum of the emerald's crystal structure — a stable, material-grounded coherence property). "Grue" is a linguistic construct that concatenates two different coherence states with a time-indexed switch. The switch at t has no grounding in any physical coherence boundary — nothing in the emerald's coherence field changes at t. Grue is a coordinate artifact: a complex description that looks like a predicate but tracks no coherent physical phase.

5. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Induction reliability tracks measurable coherence: a composite measure of system coherence should predict the timescale over which inductive generalizations remain reliable — independently of domain, complexity, or historical base rate.
  • Grue-type predicates fail to track coherence field boundaries: projectibility judgments should correlate with whether the predicate tracks real physical boundaries.
  • Induction failure is predictable from pre-transition coherence signals: declining coherence (financial volatility clustering, ecological early warning signals) should predict imminent induction failure windows.

6. Conclusion

The problem of induction dissolves when the question is shifted from logic to physics. Induction is not logically justified — Hume was right about that. It is physically grounded: it works because the universe is coherent and coherence persists. Reliability is determined by the coherence persistence time of the system being predicted. Goodman's riddle dissolves separately: projectible predicates track coherent phases; non-projectible predicates are coordinate artifacts with no phase-boundary grounding.
