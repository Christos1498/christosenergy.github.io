Abstract

The void problem encompasses a set of discrepancies between ΛCDM predictions and observations of cosmic voids — the large underdense regions occupying ~80% of the universe's volume. Specific tensions include: void density profiles (simulated voids sometimes too empty or with incorrect internal structure), void ellipticities (observed voids more elongated than simulated), void galaxy populations (luminosity functions do not always match predictions), and void velocity flows (velocity divergence inside voids differs between simulations and observations). This paper proposes a coherence-based resolution. Cosmic voids are not merely underdense regions — they are coherence-collapsed zones where local coherence C(x) has dropped below a critical threshold C_void. In these zones, the Saturnalia return current dominates: observable matter is suppressed while the return channel contains compressed, low-coherence matter that contributes to gravitational dynamics without emitting light. The observed void density is not the total density — it is the Christos-current fraction of the total.

2. Voids as Coherence-Collapsed Zones

2.1 The Coherence Threshold for Voids

In the CTF framework, a void is defined by coherence, not density. A region where C(x) drops below C_void ≈ 0.3 enters the Saturnalia-dominated regime: ordinary matter is suppressed while the return channel carries the gravitational mass. The observable density contrast:

δ_obs(x) = δ_total(x) · exp(−C_void / C(x))

The true density δ_total (including return-channel matter) is only moderately underdense (δ_total ≈ −0.5), while the observed density is much lower (δ_obs ≈ −0.9) due to coherence suppression of the observable fraction. Voids appear emptier than they are because we see only the Christos-current matter.

2.2 Return-Channel Matter in Voids

The return channel in voids contains: compressed, low-temperature gas below the emission threshold for UV/X-ray surveys; ionized gas contributing to FRB dispersion measures (testable); low-coherence dark matter in decoherent orbital configurations; and partially stripped material from voids' progenitor filaments. This matter contributes to gravitational dynamics without appearing in photometric surveys.

3. Void Ellipticity and Toroidal Alignment

Standard simulations predict near-spherical voids (ellipticity ε ~ 0.2–0.3) because dark matter-only dynamics produce isotropic evacuation. Observed voids are systematically more elongated (ε ~ 0.4–0.5) and aligned with surrounding cosmic web filaments. CTF interpretation: the Saturnalia return current preferentially flows along the toroidal axis of the cosmic coherence field — the same axis aligned with the large-scale filament network. Voids form where this return current drains matter most efficiently, which is along specific geometric directions determined by the toroidal field. The ellipticity prediction:

  • Voids aligned with the cosmic toroidal axis should show high ellipticity (ε ~ 0.5–0.6) with major axis along the nearest filament.
  • Voids perpendicular to the toroidal axis should be more spherical (ε ~ 0.2–0.3).
  • The distribution of void ellipticities should be bimodal — two populations corresponding to aligned and perpendicular orientations.

4. Void Velocity Flows and the Expansion Anomaly

The velocity divergence θ = ∇·v inside voids measures the expansion rate. Return-channel matter contributes to the gravitational potential of the void but not to the observable velocity field (because it is in a different coherence state). The same gravitational potential drives a larger expansion of the observable component:

θ_obs = θ_total · f_Christos(C) ≈ 0.35 × θ_total

If f_Christos ≈ 0.35–0.50, the observed expansion rate would be 50–65% of the total — consistent with the observed 60–80% of Hubble. The velocity divergence anomaly is a coherence-weighted measurement, not a fundamental deviation from ΛCDM gravity.

5. Void Galaxies as Residual Coherence Populations

Galaxies in voids are systematically low-luminosity, low star formation rate, and often gas-rich but inefficient at converting gas to stars. The CTF interpretation: void galaxies form in regions of low coherence (the Christos-current residual within voids). Their star formation is suppressed by the same coherence threshold described in PR-063. Prediction: void galaxy star formation rates should be suppressed relative to field galaxies of the same gas mass, not just the same stellar mass. The suppression should correlate with void-centric distance (deeper in the void = lower coherence = stronger suppression).

6. ISW Amplitude-Coherence Correlation

Supervoids produce cold spots in the CMB through the integrated Sachs-Wolfe (ISW) effect. If return-channel matter is present, it contributes to the potential but is not in the standard observable baryon distribution. The CTF prediction: the ISW cold spot amplitude should be systematically lower than ΛCDM predicts for a given observed density contrast, because the total potential (including return-channel matter) is deeper than the observable density suggests — leading to partial cancellation of the ISW signal.

7. Falsifiable Predictions

  • Bimodal void ellipticity distribution: DESI, Euclid, and 4MOST void catalogs should show two populations — high-ellipticity voids aligned with filaments and low-ellipticity voids perpendicular to filaments — not a unimodal distribution.
  • Velocity divergence-density offset: The observed relation between void density contrast and velocity divergence should be systematically offset from ΛCDM simulations by a factor consistent with f_Christos ~ 0.35–0.50 — distinguishable with precision RSD measurements from DESI.
  • Void galaxy star formation suppression: H I observations (ALFALFA, WALLABY) should show star formation rates suppressed relative to field galaxies matched in gas mass, with suppression increasing toward void centers.
  • ISW amplitude: Cross-correlation of void catalogs with Planck CMB should show ISW cold spot amplitudes systematically below ΛCDM predictions for observed density contrasts.

8. Conclusion

Cosmic voids are not simply underdense regions — they are coherence-collapsed zones where the Saturnalia return current dominates and the observable Christos-channel matter is a minority fraction of the total. The discrepancies between ΛCDM predictions and void observations — density contrast, ellipticity, velocity divergence, galaxy populations — all have a single geometric explanation: we are observing only the high-coherence fraction of a gravitationally active total density that includes return-channel matter we cannot directly see. Voids are not empty. They are filled with the return current — the unseen flow that completes the cosmic torus and shapes the geometry of underdense space.
