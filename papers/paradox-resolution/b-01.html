Multi-Scale Regulatory Synchronization, Systems Immunology, and the Coherence Threshold Model

Abstract

The immune system discriminates self from non-self through molecular recognition mechanisms that are well-established: MHC-restricted antigen presentation, clonal selection, central and peripheral tolerance, cytokine signaling, checkpoint regulation. This paper proposes a complementary systems-level interpretation within the Christos Theoretical Framework: immune recognition operates within a continuously shifting organism-wide physiological state, and this state — indexed by autonomic coherence, inflammatory tone, circadian alignment, and metabolic stability — determines the effective discrimination threshold. Autoimmune disease reflects threshold instability driven by multi-scale physiological dysregulation. Cancer immune evasion reflects the adoption of physiological signaling patterns compatible with tolerated host states. The framework does not replace molecular immunology. It proposes a systems-level regulatory layer that explains systematic phenomena molecular recognition alone cannot fully account for: tissue-specific autoimmune vulnerability, stress-sensitivity of disease flares, and the variability in immunotherapy response. All primary claims are grounded in established psychoneuroimmunology, autonomic regulation, and systems immunology literature.

2. The Coherence Threshold Model

Immune discrimination is not a fixed molecular gate — it is a dynamically adjusted threshold that shifts with the organism's overall physiological state. Define the immune activation threshold R as a function of aggregate physiological mismatch x:

R(x) = x^n / [x^n + x_crit^n]

The threshold x_crit shifts with the organism's regulatory state — higher under inflammatory load, lower under robust autonomic coherence. Operational proxies for the regulatory state include: HRV (autonomic coherence), inflammatory cytokine profiles (IL-6, TNF-α, CRP), circadian cortisol rhythm, and allostatic load index.

3. Autoimmunity as Threshold Dysregulation

Autoimmune disease is interpreted as chronic threshold dysregulation driven by multi-scale physiological dysregulation. Contributing factors with established literature support:

  • Chronic sympathetic activation (stress, insomnia, social isolation) → elevated cortisol → altered cytokine balance → shifted immune threshold (Sapolsky 2004; McEwen 2007)
  • Circadian disruption → impaired immune cell trafficking and cytokine timing → loss of temporal gating of immune responses (Scheiermann et al. 2013)
  • Metabolic dysregulation → altered T-cell differentiation and inflammatory macrophage phenotype (O'Neill & Pearce 2016)
  • Autonomic imbalance → impaired cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway → sustained inflammatory tone (Tracey 2002)

The tissue-specificity of autoimmune disease — why thyroid, islets, or synovium rather than all tissues simultaneously — is explained by differential physiological coupling: high-coherence tissues (those with tight autonomic innervation, circadian sensitivity, or metabolic precision) are most sensitive to threshold shifts.

4. Cancer Immune Evasion as Physiological Integration

Cold tumors — those with low immune infiltration despite high mutational burden — are interpreted as having adopted physiological signaling patterns compatible with the host's tolerated regulatory state. The known molecular mechanisms (PD-L1, MHC loss, antigen escape, T-cell exhaustion) are the molecular implementation of this regulatory integration. The CTF framing explains why these molecular mechanisms cluster together in cold tumors: they collectively achieve physiological compatibility with the tolerated host state.

5. Falsifiable Predictions

  • HRV predicts autoimmune flare onset: Prospective study monitoring HRV in established autoimmune patients (RA, lupus, MS) should find that HRV decline precedes clinical flare by 7–14 days — providing a predictive biomarker independent of standard inflammatory markers.
  • Circadian disruption protocol increases autoimmune biomarkers: Controlled circadian disruption in healthy subjects with genetic autoimmune risk should produce measurable increases in autoimmune-relevant antibody titers and inflammatory markers within 6 weeks.
  • HRV-targeted intervention reduces disease activity: A randomized trial of HRV biofeedback in active autoimmune disease should produce measurable reduction in disease activity scores (DAS-28 for RA, SLEDAI for lupus) independent of immunosuppressive medication changes.
  • Cold vs. hot tumor physiological signatures differ: Matched pairs of cold and hot tumors from the same cancer type should show measurable differences in local inflammatory mediator profiles, regulatory T-cell density, and metabolic signatures consistent with differential physiological integration.

6. Limitations

  • No direct measurement of organism-wide regulatory state currently exists. HRV, cytokines, and allostatic load are indirect proxies.
  • The framework is organizational and interpretive — it does not provide new molecular mechanisms, only a systems-level framing for known ones.
  • Causal direction is often unclear: physiological dysregulation may be consequence as well as cause of autoimmune disease.

7. Conclusion

The immune self/non-self problem has two complementary levels of resolution. The molecular level is established. The systems level — proposed here — interprets immune discrimination as threshold-regulated by organism-wide physiological coherence. Autoimmunity is threshold dysregulation. Cancer immune evasion is physiological integration. The framework is grounded entirely in established psychoneuroimmunology, systems immunology, and autonomic regulation literature.
