Abstract

This paper compares blockchain technology, a genuine, deployed innovation in distributed consensus, against a proposed alternative, the Field-Chain (implemented as the Resonance Ledger within the CQI), across nine architectural dimensions: consensus, security, identity, energy, scalability, value basis, attack surface, speed, and the user relationship. The blockchain side of this comparison describes real, well-documented technology and real, well-documented security incidents.

The Field-Chain side describes a proposed system whose central security claim, physical rather than mathematical trust, depends on hardware (a physically measured crystal coherence signature) that has not been built. The source material's own deployment roadmap confirms this directly, stating that coherence signatures are currently "software-simulated" rather than physically enforced. This page presents that admission prominently rather than repeating the paper's "cannot be attacked" language as established fact throughout.

I. Mathematical Trust vs. Physical Trust

The paper's account of blockchain's real limitation is accurate: every deployed blockchain consensus mechanism (Proof of Work, Proof of Stake, and their variants) does rest on computational or capital resources that could, in principle, be concentrated by a sufficiently resourced actor, and this is a genuine, widely discussed structural property of mathematical consensus, not a flaw specific to any one implementation. The paper proposes an alternative grounded in a crystal's spectral signature as an unforgeable physical identity, with security enforced by physics rather than cryptography. Its own proposed deployment roadmap describes this in phases: the current phase runs on software-simulated coherence signatures, with physical enforcement beginning only once the crystal and node hardware developed in the companion papers AN-01 and AN-09 exists. Whether phi-ratio crystal spectral signatures are in fact unforgeable and reliably measurable at the precision this architecture would require has not been demonstrated on that hardware.

II. The Nine-Dimension Comparison

The blockchain figures cited throughout this comparison are real and reasonably accurate as of the source material's writing: Bitcoin's Proof-of-Work energy consumption in the range of roughly 150 TWh per year, transaction throughput of roughly 7 TPS for Bitcoin and 15–30 TPS for Ethereum against Visa's roughly 24,000 TPS, and Shor's algorithm's real, well-established theoretical threat to RSA and elliptic-curve cryptography once sufficiently large quantum computers exist. The cited security incidents are real and well documented: the DAO hack, the Ronin and Wormhole bridge exploits, and the Mt. Gox and FTX collapses are each genuine, publicly reported events in blockchain history.

III. The CBC Economy

The paper proposes a Coherence-Based Currency in which value is created by measured coherence contribution rather than mining or staking, including a proposed Universal Basic Coherence allocation to every connected person. This is the same proposed currency system discussed in the companion paper AN-01; none of it has been built, and its viability as an actual economic system, including its proposed deflationary and anti-concentration mechanisms, has not been tested. This page does not repeat that treatment in full here.

IV. A Proposed Transition Path

The paper proposes a non-disruptive migration path in which a "Reputation Oracle" would expose a coherence score to real, existing blockchain networks (Ethereum, Solana, Bitcoin, Polygon are named specifically) via zero-knowledge proof, allowing real DeFi, DAO, and NFT systems to query it without adopting Proof of Coherence themselves, ahead of a proposed later transition to native Field-Chain transactions as physical hardware becomes available. This bridge concept is a reasonable systems-design proposal for how an unbuilt system might integrate with real infrastructure; no such oracle has been built, and the complete API and cross-chain integration specification is not disclosed in this public version.
